# Restaurant Bank Reconciliation: Monthly Process and Best Practices

Bank reconciliation compares your internal records to bank statements, catching errors, identifying fraud, and ensuring financial accuracy. For restaurants with high transaction volumes and multiple payment types, regular reconciliation is essential but often neglected. A systematic monthly process protects your business and provides confidence in your numbers.

This guide explains how to perform effective bank reconciliation for restaurant operations.

## Why Bank Reconciliation Matters

### The Purpose of Reconciliation

**Verify Accuracy**: Confirm your records match actual bank activity.
- Catch recording errors
- Identify missing transactions
- Ensure accurate cash position

**Detect Fraud**: Find unauthorized transactions.
- Employee theft
- Vendor fraud
- Unauthorized charges

**Maintain Control**: Systematic oversight of cash.
- Regular review of all transactions
- Timely identification of issues
- Audit trail for accountability

### Restaurant-Specific Challenges

**High Volume**: Many transactions daily.
- Cash deposits
- Credit card settlements
- ACH payments
- Check payments
- Automatic debits

**Multiple Revenue Streams**: Various sources of income.
- In-house sales
- Third-party delivery platforms
- Catering
- Gift card settlements

**Timing Differences**: Not all transactions settle same day.
- Credit card batches
- Pending deposits
- Outstanding checks

### Consequences of Neglecting Reconciliation

**Financial Statement Errors**: Inaccurate cash balance.
- Overstated or understated cash
- Wrong profit/loss numbers
- Poor decision-making from bad data

**Undetected Fraud**: Problems grow over time.
- Small theft goes unnoticed
- Builds to significant losses
- Harder to investigate later

**Cash Management Issues**: Don't know true cash position.
- Overdrafts and fees
- Missed opportunities
- Poor planning

## Understanding the Reconciliation Process

### What You're Reconciling

**Your Records**: Internal accounting system.
- General ledger cash balance
- Recorded deposits
- Recorded payments
- Expected ending balance

**Bank Records**: Bank statement.
- Statement beginning balance
- Deposits per bank
- Withdrawals per bank
- Statement ending balance

### Why They Differ

**Timing Differences**: Transactions recorded at different times.
- Outstanding checks: You recorded, bank hasn't processed
- Deposits in transit: You deposited, not yet credited
- Bank fees: Bank charged, you haven't recorded

**Errors**: Mistakes on either side.
- Your recording errors
- Bank errors (rare but happen)
- Amount discrepancies

### The Goal

**Reconciled Balance**: Both records agree after adjustments.
- Your adjusted balance = Bank adjusted balance
- Differences identified and explained
- Corrections made as needed

## Step-by-Step Reconciliation Guide

### Preparation

**Gather Materials**:
1. Bank statement for period
2. General ledger cash account detail
3. Previous month's reconciliation
4. Outstanding items from prior month
5. Deposit slips and check images if needed

**Set Up Workspace**:
- Quiet time without interruptions
- Consistent schedule (same time monthly)
- Checklist to follow

### Step 1: Verify Beginning Balance

**Compare**:
- Your beginning cash balance
- Bank statement beginning balance
- Previous month's reconciliation ending balance

**Should Match**: If not, research prior month's reconciliation.

### Step 2: Identify Deposits

**List All Deposits**:
- Pull deposits from your records
- Pull deposits from bank statement
- Match each deposit

**Common Issues**:
- Deposits in transit (you recorded, bank hasn't credited)
- Deposits recorded wrong amount
- Deposits posted different date

**Mark as Reconciled**: Check off matched items.

### Step 3: Identify Payments/Withdrawals

**List All Payments**:
- Checks from your records
- ACH payments
- Automatic debits
- Bank fees and charges

**Match to Bank Statement**:
- Check each item off
- Note outstanding checks (not yet cashed)
- Identify bank charges not yet recorded

### Step 4: Prepare Reconciliation

**Bank Statement Balance**: Start with ending balance.

**Add**:
- Deposits in transit (you deposited, bank hasn't credited)

**Subtract**:
- Outstanding checks (you issued, bank hasn't processed)

**Equals**: Adjusted bank balance

---

**Book Balance**: Start with your ending cash balance.

**Add**:
- Interest earned (on statement, not in books)
- Credits you haven't recorded

**Subtract**:
- Bank fees not recorded
- Returned items
- Debits you haven't recorded

**Equals**: Adjusted book balance

### Step 5: Compare Adjusted Balances

**They Should Match**:
- Adjusted bank balance = Adjusted book balance

**If They Match**: Reconciliation complete.

**If They Don't Match**: Find the difference.

### Step 6: Make Journal Entries

**Record Items to Books**:
- Bank fees
- Interest income
- Errors discovered
- Other adjustments

**Entry Example** (bank fees):
```
Debit: Bank Fees Expense    $35
Credit: Cash                $35
```

## Common Reconciliation Issues

### Credit Card Settlements

**The Challenge**: POS shows one total, bank shows another.

**Causes**:
- Processing fees deducted
- Timing of batch settlement
- Chargebacks processed

**Solution**:
- Reconcile gross deposits to POS totals
- Track credit card fees separately
- Monitor chargeback activity

### Third-Party Delivery Platforms

**The Challenge**: Multiple platforms with different payment schedules.

**Causes**:
- Weekly payments from platforms
- Commissions deducted
- Adjustments and refunds

**Solution**:
- Track each platform separately
- Reconcile to platform statements
- Record gross revenue and commissions

### Cash Handling Discrepancies

**The Challenge**: Cash deposit doesn't match expected.

**Causes**:
- Count errors
- Theft
- Recording errors
- Till management issues

**Solution**:
- Daily cash reconciliation
- Over/short tracking
- Investigate significant variances
- Improve cash handling procedures

### Outstanding Checks

**The Challenge**: Checks that never clear.

**Considerations**:
- Checks over 90 days old
- Lost or forgotten checks
- Vendor hasn't deposited

**Solution**:
- Follow up on stale checks
- Void and reissue if needed
- Write off after appropriate period
- Follow state escheatment laws

### Unidentified Deposits/Withdrawals

**The Challenge**: Items on statement you don't recognize.

**Steps**:
1. Check all transaction sources
2. Review contracts for auto-debits
3. Contact bank for details if needed
4. Research thoroughly before assuming error

## Point of Sale Reconciliation

### Daily POS to Deposit Reconciliation

**Process**:
1. Pull daily sales report from POS
2. Separate cash, credit, and other tenders
3. Count cash and verify to POS
4. Verify credit card batch total
5. Document and explain differences

**Sample Daily Reconciliation**:
```
Date: October 15

POS Reported Sales:        $3,456.78

By Tender Type:
  Cash                       $567.89
  Credit Cards             $2,678.89
  Gift Cards                 $210.00
                           ---------
  Total                    $3,456.78

Cash Verification:
  Till Count                 $867.89
  Opening Bank              ($300.00)
  Cash Sales                 $567.89  ✓

Credit Card Verification:
  Batch Settlement         $2,678.89  ✓

Variance:                      $0.00
```

### Credit Card Processing Reconciliation

**What to Verify**:
- POS batch totals to processor statement
- Processor deposits to bank account
- Processing fees to fee schedule

**Common Issues**:
- Tips added after authorization
- Voids and refunds
- Chargebacks
- Fee variations

## Best Practices

### Timing and Frequency

**Monthly Reconciliation**: Within 10 days of statement.
- Fresh memory of transactions
- Timely error detection
- Manageable workload

**Daily Procedures**: Set up for monthly success.
- Daily cash reconciliation
- Daily batch verification
- Exception handling immediately

### Segregation of Duties

**Ideal Separation**:
- Person A: Prepares deposits
- Person B: Records transactions
- Person C: Performs reconciliation
- Owner: Reviews completed reconciliation

**Small Restaurant**: At minimum, owner reviews.
- Someone other than deposit handler reconciles
- Bank statements sent to owner
- Regular review and sign-off

### Documentation

**Keep Records Of**:
- Completed reconciliations
- Supporting documentation
- Journal entries made
- Explanation of adjustments

**Retention**: 3-7 years depending on requirements.

### Using Technology

**Accounting Software Features**:
- Bank feed integration
- Automatic matching
- Reconciliation reports
- Historical tracking

**Benefits**:
- Faster reconciliation
- Fewer errors
- Better audit trail
- Real-time visibility

### Handling Errors

**When You Find Errors**:
1. Determine if your error or bank's
2. Make correcting entry if yours
3. Contact bank if theirs
4. Document the resolution
5. Improve process to prevent recurrence

## Key Takeaways

Bank reconciliation ensures financial accuracy and control:

1. **Reconcile monthly**: Within 10 days of receiving statement.

2. **Understand the process**: Match your records to bank records, explain differences.

3. **Watch for fraud**: Reconciliation is key fraud detection control.

4. **Address timing differences**: Deposits in transit and outstanding checks are normal.

5. **Reconcile POS daily**: Catch issues before monthly close.

6. **Segregate duties**: Different people should handle cash and reconciliation.

7. **Document thoroughly**: Keep records of all reconciliations and adjustments.

8. **Use technology**: Software makes reconciliation faster and more accurate.

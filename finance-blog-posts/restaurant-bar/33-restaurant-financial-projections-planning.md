# Restaurant Financial Projections: Creating Realistic Business Plans

Financial projections translate your restaurant concept into numbers that demonstrate viability. Whether for investors, lenders, or your own planning, well-crafted projections show expected revenue, expenses, and profitability. Realistic projections require industry knowledge, market research, and conservative assumptions.

This guide covers creating financial projections for restaurant ventures.

## Purpose of Financial Projections

### Who Uses Projections

**Investors**: To evaluate return potential.
- Is the opportunity attractive?
- What's the expected timeline?
- What are the risks?

**Lenders**: To assess repayment ability.
- Can the business service debt?
- Is there adequate cushion?
- What's the collateral?

**You**: To plan and evaluate.
- Is the concept viable?
- What sales are needed to break even?
- What capital is required?

### Types of Projections

**Start-Up Projections**: For new restaurants.
- Pre-opening costs
- Ramp-up period
- Stabilized operations

**Operating Projections**: For existing restaurants.
- Continuation of current operations
- Impact of changes
- Growth planning

**Expansion Projections**: For growth.
- Additional locations
- Capital requirements
- Timeline to profitability

## Revenue Projections

### Approaches to Revenue Forecasting

**Capacity-Based**:
```
Seats: 80
Turns per day: 2.5 lunch + 1.5 dinner = 4.0
Days open: 350
Annual covers: 112,000
Average check: $35
Annual revenue: $3,920,000
```

**Comparable-Based**:
- Research similar restaurants
- Use industry data
- Adjust for market differences

**Bottom-Up**:
- Estimate by daypart
- By revenue stream
- By customer segment

### Key Revenue Drivers

**Capacity Factors**:
- Seats
- Table turns
- Days/hours of operation

**Average Check**:
- Menu prices
- Beverage attachment
- Appetizers/desserts
- Per-person average

**Traffic Drivers**:
- Location quality
- Marketing effectiveness
- Competition
- Concept appeal

### Monthly Revenue Build-Up

**Year 1 Monthly Projection**:
```
Month 1-2: Soft opening (60% of stabilized)
Month 3-4: Ramp-up (75% of stabilized)
Month 5-6: Building (85% of stabilized)
Month 7-9: Approaching stabilized (95%)
Month 10-12: Stabilized operations (100%)
```

### Seasonality

**Consider**:
- Local seasonal patterns
- Holiday impacts
- Weather effects
- Competition calendar

**Example Seasonal Adjustment**:
```
January: 85% of average
February: 85%
March-May: 100%
June-August: 110%
September-November: 100%
December: 120%
```

## Expense Projections

### Fixed vs. Variable Expenses

**Fixed Costs**: Same regardless of sales.
- Rent
- Insurance
- Base utilities
- Loan payments
- Core management

**Variable Costs**: Change with sales.
- Food and beverage cost
- Hourly labor
- Credit card fees
- Some utilities

**Semi-Variable**: Have fixed and variable components.
- Labor (minimum staffing + volume-based)
- Utilities (base charge + usage)

### Cost of Goods Sold

**Projecting Food Cost**:
- Target percentage: 28-35%
- Based on menu pricing strategy
- Account for waste

**Projecting Beverage Cost**:
- Target percentage: 18-24%
- Varies by beverage mix
- Based on program design

### Labor Costs

**Components**:
- Management salaries
- Hourly wages
- Payroll taxes (add 8-10%)
- Benefits if provided

**Target Percentage**: 28-35% of revenue.

**Build-Up Method**:
```
Management: $8,000/month fixed
Hourly (varies with volume):
  - Minimum staffing: $15,000/month
  - Additional per $10,000 revenue: $3,000
```

### Occupancy Costs

**Components**:
- Base rent
- CAM/NNN charges
- Property taxes (if tenant responsibility)
- Insurance (property portion)

**Target**: 6-10% of revenue.

### Operating Expenses

**Categories**:
- Utilities: 2-4%
- Marketing: 1-3%
- Supplies: 1-2%
- Credit card fees: 2-3%
- Repairs: 1-2%
- Professional services: 0.5-1%

### Year 1 Monthly Projection

**Detail by Month**: Shows ramp-up.

### Years 2-5 Annual Projection

**Show Growth Path**:
- Reasonable revenue growth
- Margin improvements
- Stabilized operations

### Cash Flow Projection

**Different from P&L**: Shows actual cash.

**Include**:
- Capital expenditures
- Loan principal payments
- Working capital changes
- Owner draws/distributions

### Break-Even Analysis

**Calculate Break-Even Sales**:
```
Fixed Costs: $30,000/month
Variable Cost %: 55%
Contribution Margin: 45%
Break-Even: $30,000 ÷ 0.45 = $66,667/month
```

## Testing Your Assumptions

### Sensitivity Analysis

**What If Scenarios**:
- Revenue 10% below projection
- Food cost 2% higher
- Labor 2% higher

**Impact Assessment**: How much cushion exists?

### Common Assumption Errors

**Over-Optimistic**:
- Faster ramp-up than realistic
- Higher average check than market supports
- Lower costs than industry standards
- No contingency for problems

**Realistic Approach**:
- Conservative revenue
- Industry-standard costs
- Adequate contingency
- Delayed profitability

### Validating Assumptions

**Sources**:
- Industry benchmarks
- Comparable restaurants
- Vendor quotes
- Local market research

### Reasonableness Tests

**Check Against Benchmarks**:
| Metric | Your Projection | Benchmark |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| Food Cost % | 30% | 28-35% |
| Labor Cost % | 32% | 28-35% |
| Prime Cost % | 62% | 60-65% |
| Occupancy % | 8% | 6-10% |
| Net Margin | 6% | 3-10% |

## Presenting Projections

### What to Include

**Executive Summary**: Key highlights.
- Investment required
- Projected revenue
- Break-even timeline
- Expected returns

**Detailed Projections**:
- Monthly Year 1
- Annual Years 2-5
- Assumptions documented

**Supporting Analysis**:
- Market research
- Comparable analysis
- Sensitivity scenarios

### Common Questions

**Be Prepared For**:
- How did you determine revenue?
- What if sales are 20% lower?
- Why is your food cost different from industry?
- When do you break even?
- What's your worst-case scenario?

### Credibility Factors

**What Builds Confidence**:
- Detailed assumptions documented
- Industry experience evident
- Conservative approach
- Sensitivity analysis included
- Market research supporting

**What Raises Concerns**:
- Vague assumptions
- Above-average margins without explanation
- No contingency
- Unrealistic ramp-up
- Ignoring seasonality

## Key Takeaways

Realistic financial projections demonstrate viability:

1. **Revenue from capacity**: Seats × turns × check × days.

2. **Account for ramp-up**: New restaurants don't hit targets immediately.

3. **Use industry benchmarks**: For expense percentages.

4. **Test with scenarios**: What happens if assumptions are wrong?

5. **Show break-even**: Critical milestone for lenders and investors.

6. **Document assumptions**: Transparency builds credibility.

7. **Be conservative**: Under-promise and over-deliver.

8. **Include cash flow**: Different from profitability.

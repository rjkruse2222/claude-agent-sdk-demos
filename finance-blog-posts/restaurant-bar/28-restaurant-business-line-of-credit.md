# Restaurant Business Lines of Credit: When and How to Use Them

A business line of credit provides flexible access to funds when you need them, making it a valuable tool for restaurant cash flow management. Unlike term loans with fixed disbursement and payment schedules, lines of credit let you borrow, repay, and borrow again—paying interest only on what you use.

This guide explains how restaurant lines of credit work and when to use them.

## Understanding Lines of Credit

### What Is a Business Line of Credit?

A line of credit is pre-approved borrowing capacity from a lender. You can draw funds up to your limit, repay them, and draw again—similar to a credit card but typically with better terms.

**Key Characteristics**:
- **Credit Limit**: Maximum amount available
- **Draw Period**: Time you can access funds
- **Repayment Terms**: How you pay back draws
- **Interest Rate**: Cost of borrowed funds
- **Revolving**: Repaid amounts become available again

### Lines of Credit vs. Term Loans

| Feature | Line of Credit | Term Loan |
|---------|---------------|-----------|
| Access | Draw as needed | Lump sum at closing |
| Interest | Only on amount used | On full loan amount |
| Repayment | Flexible/revolving | Fixed schedule |
| Best For | Short-term/variable needs | Fixed, known expenses |
| Availability | Ongoing (revolving) | One-time |

### Secured vs. Unsecured Lines

**Secured Line of Credit**:
- Backed by collateral (equipment, inventory, receivables)
- Lower interest rates
- Higher credit limits
- More qualification requirements

**Unsecured Line of Credit**:
- No collateral required
- Higher interest rates
- Lower credit limits
- Easier application process

## How Restaurant Lines of Credit Work

### The Draw Process

**When You Need Funds**:
1. Request draw (online, phone, or check)
2. Funds transfer to your account
3. Interest accrues on outstanding balance
4. Make payments per terms
5. Repaid amounts available to draw again

**Example**:
- Credit line: $50,000
- Draw $15,000 in January
- Interest accrues on $15,000
- Pay down $10,000 in February
- Available: $45,000
- Outstanding: $5,000

### Interest Calculation

**Interest Only on Outstanding Balance**:
- $50,000 line at 10% APR
- Draw $20,000
- Monthly interest: $20,000 × 10% ÷ 12 = $167
- Not $50,000 × 10% ÷ 12 = $417

**Variable vs. Fixed Rates**:
- Most lines have variable rates
- Tied to prime rate plus spread
- Rate changes affect interest cost

### Typical Terms

**Credit Limits**: $10,000 to $500,000+
- Based on revenue and credit
- May increase with relationship

**Interest Rates**: 7-25%
- Depends on credit quality
- Secured lines lower than unsecured

**Draw Period**: 1-5 years typically
- Period during which you can draw
- May need to renew

**Repayment**:
- Interest-only payments common
- Some require principal payment
- Full repayment at draw period end

**Fees**:
- Annual fee: $0-$500
- Draw fees: Sometimes
- Maintenance fees: Varies

## When to Use a Line of Credit

### Ideal Uses

**Seasonal Cash Flow Gaps**:
- Slow January after holiday rush
- Summer slowdown if applicable
- Bridge between busy seasons

**Inventory Purchases**:
- Stock up for busy period
- Take advantage of bulk pricing
- Cover vendor payment timing

**Unexpected Expenses**:
- Equipment repairs
- Emergency staffing
- Unplanned opportunities

**Payroll Smoothing**:
- Cover payroll during slow weeks
- Bridge until deposits arrive
- Timing between revenue and expenses

### When NOT to Use a Line of Credit

**Long-Term Needs**:
- Equipment purchases (use equipment loan)
- Renovation (use term loan or SBA)
- Expansion (use appropriate financing)

**Ongoing Operating Losses**:
- Line of credit won't fix unprofitable business
- May create deeper debt problems
- Address underlying issues first

**As Permanent Working Capital**:
- If you always need the line maxed out
- Indicates undercapitalization
- Consider equity or term debt instead

### Good vs. Bad Uses

**Good Uses**:
- Cover seasonal dip, repay within 3 months
- Stock up for holiday, repay from holiday sales
- Emergency repair, repay over 6 months

**Bad Uses**:
- Cover ongoing operating losses
- Finance long-term equipment
- Never plan to pay down

## Qualifying for a Line of Credit

### What Lenders Evaluate

**Business Credit and History**:
- Time in business (2+ years preferred)
- Business credit score
- Payment history with vendors
- Industry risk factors

**Personal Credit**:
- Owner's credit score (650+ preferred)
- Personal credit history
- May require personal guarantee

**Financial Performance**:
- Annual revenue ($100,000+ typically)
- Profitability trends
- Cash flow adequacy
- Debt levels

**Collateral** (for secured lines):
- Equipment value
- Inventory
- Accounts receivable
- Real estate

### Documentation Required

**Typical Requirements**:
- Business tax returns (2-3 years)
- Personal tax returns (2-3 years)
- Bank statements (3-12 months)
- Profit and loss statement
- Balance sheet
- Business licenses
- Lease agreement

### Improving Your Chances

**Before Applying**:
- Improve personal credit score
- Reduce existing debt
- Show consistent revenue
- Clean up financial records

**Build Banking Relationship**:
- Existing relationship helps
- Business checking account
- History of deposits

## Where to Get a Line of Credit

### Traditional Banks

**Characteristics**:
- Lower interest rates
- Higher requirements
- Longer approval process
- Relationship-based

**Best For**: Established restaurants with good financials.

**Typical Terms**:
- Rates: Prime + 1-5%
- Limits: $25,000-$500,000+
- Time in business: 2+ years

### Credit Unions

**Characteristics**:
- Competitive rates
- More personal service
- Member requirements
- Community focus

**Best For**: Local restaurants with community ties.

### Online Lenders

**Characteristics**:
- Faster approval (days vs. weeks)
- Higher rates
- Lower requirements
- Convenient process

**Best For**: Newer restaurants or those needing quick access.

**Typical Terms**:
- Rates: 10-25%+
- Limits: $5,000-$250,000
- Time in business: 6+ months

### SBA CAPLines

**What They Are**: SBA-backed lines of credit.

**Types**:
- Seasonal CAPLine: Seasonal working capital
- Contract CAPLine: Contract-related expenses
- Working Capital CAPLine: Short-term needs

**Benefits**:
- Government guarantee reduces lender risk
- Better terms possible
- Longer draw periods

**Requirements**:
- More documentation
- Longer approval process
- Must meet SBA eligibility

## Managing Your Line Effectively

### Draw Strategy

**Borrow Only What You Need**:
- Don't draw "just in case"
- Calculate actual need
- Minimize interest cost

**Have a Repayment Plan**:
- Know how you'll repay before drawing
- Timeline for payback
- Source of repayment funds

### Payment Management

**More Than Minimums When Possible**:
- Interest-only minimums don't reduce principal
- Extra payments reduce interest cost
- Build available credit faster

**Systematic Paydown**:
- Weekly payments match restaurant cash flow
- Reduce average balance
- Lower overall interest cost

### Monitoring and Review

**Track Your Usage**:
- Current outstanding balance
- Available credit
- Interest being paid
- Usage trends

**Regular Review**:
- Is the line size appropriate?
- Are terms still competitive?
- Is usage pattern healthy?

### Renewal Considerations

**Before Renewal**:
- Review usage history
- Evaluate if still needed
- Shop competitive options
- Negotiate better terms if possible

**Preparing for Renewal**:
- Maintain good payment history
- Keep financials current
- Be ready to provide updated documents

## Common Pitfalls

### Treating Line as Permanent Capital

**Problem**: Always at or near maximum.

**Issue**: Line of credit becomes term debt without structure.

**Solution**: If you need permanent capital, restructure to term loan.

### Not Having a Repayment Plan

**Problem**: Draw without knowing how you'll repay.

**Issue**: Balance grows, interest compounds.

**Solution**: Before drawing, identify repayment source and timeline.

### Using for Wrong Purposes

**Problem**: Financing long-term needs with short-term debt.

**Issue**: Mismatched terms create cash flow pressure.

**Solution**: Use appropriate financing for each need.

### Ignoring Costs

**Problem**: Not tracking total interest paid.

**Issue**: Expensive financing erodes profitability.

**Solution**: Monitor interest expense; pay down aggressively.

## Alternatives to Consider

### Business Credit Cards

**Comparison**:
- Easier to obtain
- Higher rates typically
- More limited credit
- May have rewards

**Best For**: Smaller, frequent purchases; building credit.

### Merchant Cash Advances

**Comparison**:
- Fast funding
- Very expensive
- Repaid from daily sales
- No fixed payment schedule

**Best For**: Emergency only; understand true cost.

### Invoice Factoring

**Comparison**:
- Based on receivables
- Quick access
- Cost based on invoice value
- Good for B2B restaurants (catering)

**Best For**: Restaurants with significant receivables.

### Term Loans

**Comparison**:
- Fixed payment schedule
- Know total cost upfront
- Less flexibility
- Better for specific purposes

**Best For**: Known, fixed expenses; long-term needs.

## Key Takeaways

A business line of credit provides valuable flexibility for restaurant operations:

1. **Understand the product**: Revolving credit you can draw, repay, and draw again.

2. **Pay interest only on what you use**: Don't draw more than needed.

3. **Best for short-term needs**: Seasonal gaps, inventory, unexpected expenses.

4. **Not for long-term financing**: Use term loans for equipment and expansion.

5. **Have a repayment plan**: Know how you'll pay back before drawing.

6. **Maintain the relationship**: Good payment history enables better terms.

7. **Monitor costs**: Track interest expense and total cost.

8. **Get it before you need it**: Easier to qualify when business is strong.

---

*Evaluate whether a business line of credit fits your cash flow needs. Compare options from your bank, credit union, and online lenders. Apply when your business is performing well, before you urgently need the funds.*

# Restaurant Audit Preparation: How to Handle IRS and State Tax Examinations

Tax audits are stressful for any business, but restaurants face particular scrutiny due to cash transactions, tip income, and industry-specific compliance requirements. Proper preparation, organized records, and professional guidance can make the difference between a routine examination and a costly assessment. Understanding the audit process helps you respond effectively.

This guide covers audit preparation and response for restaurant businesses.

## Understanding Restaurant Audits

### Types of Audits

**Correspondence Audit**: By mail, specific issues.
- Most common type
- Limited scope
- Respond with documentation

**Office Audit**: At IRS office.
- More detailed than correspondence
- May involve interview
- Bring requested documents

**Field Audit**: At your location.
- Most comprehensive
- Examiner visits your restaurant
- Reviews records on-site

**Sales Tax Audit**: State revenue department.
- Verifies sales tax collection
- Reviews exemptions
- Checks tax remittance

### Why Restaurants Get Audited

**High-Risk Industry Factors**:
- Cash transactions common
- Tip income underreporting
- Complex payroll situations
- High failure rate
- Historical compliance issues

**Selection Methods**:
- Computer scoring (DIF)
- Random selection
- Information matching
- Related examinations
- Industry projects

### Audit Scope

**What Examiners Review**:
- Income completeness
- Expense legitimacy
- Tip reporting compliance
- Payroll accuracy
- Sales tax collection
- Record keeping adequacy

## Common Audit Triggers

### Income-Related Triggers

**Cash Skimming Indicators**:
- Bank deposits don't match reported income
- Lifestyle inconsistent with reported income
- Cost of goods analysis shows unreported sales
- Marked bills or other evidence

**Gross Profit Analysis**:
```
Examiner calculation:
  Purchases: $300,000
  Beginning inventory: $15,000
  Ending inventory: $18,000
  COGS: $297,000
  Expected gross profit margin: 70%
  Expected sales: $990,000
  Reported sales: $900,000
  Discrepancy: $90,000 (red flag)
```

### Tip Reporting Issues

**Under-Reported Tips**:
- Tips below 8% of gross receipts
- Significant difference from credit card tip rates
- Below area or industry averages
- Inconsistent with establishment type

### Expense Deductions

**Questionable Deductions**:
- Personal expenses as business
- Inadequate documentation
- Excessive compared to industry
- Round numbers suggesting estimates

### Payroll Red Flags

**Employment Tax Issues**:
- Worker misclassification
- Unreported cash payments
- Tip credit violations
- FICA shortfalls

## Preparation Essentials

### Organizing Records

**Gather Documentation**:
```
Record categories to organize:
□ Bank statements (all accounts)
□ Deposit slips
□ Credit card statements
□ POS reports
□ Purchase invoices
□ Payroll records
□ Tip reports
□ Tax returns filed
□ Asset purchase records
□ Lease agreements
```

### Record Retention Requirements

**Keep Records For**:
| Record Type | Retention Period |
|-------------|------------------|
| Income records | 7 years |
| Expense documentation | 7 years |
| Employment tax records | 4 years |
| Tip reports | 4 years |
| Asset records | Life of asset + 7 years |

### Professional Representation

**When to Get Help**:
- Complex issues involved
- Large potential assessment
- Criminal investigation hints
- You're not comfortable

**Types of Representatives**:
- CPA (can represent before IRS)
- Enrolled Agent (tax specialist)
- Tax Attorney (for legal issues)

**Power of Attorney**: Form 2848 authorizes representation.

### Pre-Audit Internal Review

**Before Responding**:
1. Review returns being examined
2. Identify potential issues
3. Gather supporting documentation
4. Reconcile records
5. Consult with tax professional

## During the Audit

### Initial Response

**When You Receive Notice**:
1. Don't panic
2. Read carefully for scope and deadlines
3. Contact your tax professional
4. Respond timely (usually 30 days)
5. Request extension if needed

### Working with Examiners

**Professional Conduct**:
- Be courteous and professional
- Answer questions directly
- Don't volunteer information
- Provide only what's requested
- Take notes on discussions

**What NOT to Do**:
- Don't be hostile or argumentative
- Don't provide verbal estimates
- Don't make up explanations
- Don't destroy or alter records
- Don't ignore requests

### Document Requests

**Information Document Requests (IDRs)**:
- Respond to each item
- If you don't have something, explain why
- Provide copies, not originals
- Organize clearly
- Document what you provide

**Reasonable Extensions**: Examiners usually grant additional time if requested professionally.

### The Examination Process

**Field Audit Typical Flow**:
1. Opening conference
2. Tour of operation
3. Record review
4. Interviews
5. Analysis period
6. Closing conference
7. Report issued

### Protecting Your Rights

**Taxpayer Rights Include**:
- Professional treatment
- Privacy and confidentiality
- Know why information is requested
- Representation
- Appeal disagreements
- Pay only correct amount

## Responding to Findings

### Audit Report

**No Change Letter**: No adjustments—congratulations.

**Examination Report**: Proposed adjustments.
- Review carefully
- Understand each adjustment
- Identify disagreements

### Agreeing with Findings

**If Correct**:
- Sign agreement form
- Arrange payment if taxes owed
- Consider payment plans if needed
- Learn from experience

### Disputing Findings

**Options If You Disagree**:

**Informal Conference**: With examiner's manager.
- Often resolves disputes
- No additional cost
- Faster resolution

**Appeals**: IRS Office of Appeals.
- Independent review
- Settlement-oriented
- Good success rate

**Tax Court**: If appeals fail.
- More formal
- Higher cost
- Sometimes necessary

### Assessment and Payment

**If You Owe**:
- Tax amount
- Interest (mandatory)
- Penalties (may be abated)

**Payment Options**:
- Full payment
- Installment agreement
- Offer in compromise (if eligible)

## Prevention Strategies

### Record Keeping Best Practices

**Daily Records**:
- Z-tapes/daily reports
- Deposit records
- Tip reports
- Cash counts

**Monthly Reconciliation**:
- Bank reconciliation
- Sales vs. deposits
- Purchases vs. inventory
- Payroll verification

### Internal Controls

**Reduce Risk Through**:
- Separation of duties
- Regular reconciliation
- Documented procedures
- Management oversight
- POS system controls

### Common Issue Prevention

**Cash Handling**:
- All sales through register
- Daily deposit of all receipts
- Documentation of all cash paid out
- Regular cash counts

**Tip Compliance**:
- Employee training
- Written tip reporting policy
- Regular review of reported tips
- Consider TRAC agreement

**Expense Documentation**:
- Receipt for every purchase
- Business purpose documented
- Credit card statements aren't enough
- Contemporaneous records

### Annual Self-Audit

**Review Each Year**:
```
Self-audit checklist:
□ Income reconciled to bank deposits
□ Tips reported align with credit card data
□ All expenses documented
□ Payroll records complete
□ Sales tax properly collected and remitted
□ All required returns filed
□ Cash handling procedures followed
```

## Key Takeaways

Audit preparation protects your restaurant from costly assessments:

1. **Keep excellent records**: Your best defense.

2. **Respond timely**: Don't ignore notices.

3. **Get professional help**: Complex audits need experts.

4. **Be professional**: Cooperation works better than confrontation.

5. **Know your rights**: Including appeal rights.

6. **Provide only what's requested**: Don't volunteer extra information.

7. **Learn from experience**: Improve systems after audit.

8. **Prevention is better**: Good practices reduce audit risk.

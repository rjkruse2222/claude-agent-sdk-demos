# Restaurant Profit and Loss Statement: How to Read and Analyze Your P&L

The profit and loss statement (P&L) is the most important financial report for restaurant operators. It tells you whether you're making money, where it's coming from, and where it's going. Understanding how to read and analyze your P&L enables informed decisions that improve profitability.

This guide explains restaurant P&L structure and analysis techniques.

## Understanding the P&L Statement

### What Is a P&L Statement?

The P&L (also called income statement) summarizes revenues and expenses over a specific period, showing whether the business made a profit or loss.

**Basic Formula**: Revenue - Expenses = Net Income (Profit or Loss)

**Time Period**: Usually monthly, with year-to-date comparison.

### Why It Matters

**Performance Measurement**: Are you profitable?

**Trend Analysis**: Are things improving or declining?

**Decision Support**: Where to focus improvement efforts?

**Stakeholder Communication**: Report to owners, investors, lenders.

### P&L vs. Cash Flow

**P&L Shows**: Revenue earned and expenses incurred (accrual basis).

**Cash Flow Shows**: Actual cash in and out.

**Important Distinction**: Profitable on P&L doesn't mean cash-rich.
- You can have profit but no cash (paid expenses before collecting revenue)
- You can have cash but no profit (borrowed money or delayed payments)

## Restaurant P&L Structure

### Standard Format

```
REVENUE
  Food Sales
  Beverage Sales
  Other Revenue
  Total Revenue

COST OF GOODS SOLD
  Food Cost
  Beverage Cost
  Total COGS

GROSS PROFIT

OPERATING EXPENSES
  Labor Costs
  Occupancy Costs
  Other Operating Expenses
  Total Operating Expenses

OPERATING PROFIT (EBITDA)

OTHER EXPENSES
  Depreciation & Amortization
  Interest Expense

NET PROFIT BEFORE TAX

  Income Tax Expense

NET PROFIT
```

## Line-by-Line Analysis

### Revenue Section

**Food Sales**: Revenue from food menu items.
- Largest revenue component
- Track trends and seasonality
- Compare to prior periods

**Beverage Sales**: All drink revenue.
- Alcoholic and non-alcoholic
- May track separately for analysis
- Higher margins than food typically

**Other Revenue**: Non-food/beverage income.
- Catering
- Gift card breakage
- Merchandise
- Room rentals

**Key Questions**:
- Is total revenue growing?
- What's the food-to-beverage mix?
- Are there opportunities in "other"?

### Cost of Goods Sold

**Food Cost**: Direct cost of food sold.
- Target: 28-35% of food sales
- Varies by concept
- Key controllable cost

**Beverage Cost**: Direct cost of beverages sold.
- Target: 18-24% of beverage sales
- Varies by mix (beer vs. liquor)
- Higher margin than food

**Key Questions**:
- Are costs within target percentages?
- What's causing variances?
- Are there pricing or purchasing opportunities?

### Gross Profit

**Calculation**: Total Revenue - Total COGS

**Meaning**: Money left after paying for products sold.

**Target**: 65-75% for most restaurants.

**Why It Matters**: This funds all other expenses and profit.

### Labor Costs

**Management Salary**: Fixed salaries for managers.
- More fixed, less variable
- Includes benefits allocation

**Hourly Wages**: All hourly employee wages.
- Most variable labor cost
- Tied to scheduling effectiveness

**Payroll Taxes**: Employer portion of taxes.
- Typically 7-10% of wages
- Social Security, Medicare, unemployment

**Benefits**: Health insurance, retirement, etc.
- Varies widely by operation
- May be significant cost

**Total Labor Target**: 28-35% depending on service level.

### Prime Cost

**Calculation**: Total COGS + Total Labor

**Target**: 60-65% of revenue.

**Why It Matters**: These are your two largest controllable costs. Combined, they determine profitability potential.

### Operating Expenses

**Rent/Occupancy**: Fixed cost for location.
- Target: 6-10% of revenue
- Often relatively fixed
- Critical in site selection

**Utilities**: Gas, electric, water.
- Somewhat variable with volume
- Target: 2-4% of revenue

**Insurance**: Various coverage.
- General liability, property, workers comp
- Target: 1-2% of revenue

**Marketing**: Advertising and promotion.
- More discretionary
- Target: 1-3% of revenue

**Repairs & Maintenance**: Keeping things running.
- Can vary widely
- Budget 1-2% of revenue

**Credit Card Fees**: Processing costs.
- Typically 2-3% of card sales
- Rising cost center for many

### EBITDA

**Calculation**: Gross Profit - Labor - Operating Expenses

**Meaning**: Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, Amortization.

**Why It Matters**: Measures operational profitability before financing and accounting adjustments.

**Target**: 15-20% for healthy operations.

### Below-the-Line Items

**Depreciation**: Non-cash expense for asset wear.
- Required by accounting standards
- Reduces taxable income
- Doesn't affect cash flow directly

**Interest Expense**: Cost of borrowed money.
- Depends on debt level
- Fixed payment obligation

### Net Profit

**Calculation**: EBITDA - Depreciation - Interest - Taxes

**Meaning**: Bottom-line profitability.

**Target**: 5-10% for healthy restaurant.

**What It Funds**: Owner return, reinvestment, debt paydown.

## Key Ratios and Metrics

### Cost Percentages

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Food Cost % | Food Cost ÷ Food Sales | 28-35% |
| Beverage Cost % | Bev Cost ÷ Bev Sales | 18-24% |
| Labor Cost % | Total Labor ÷ Total Sales | 28-35% |
| Prime Cost % | (COGS + Labor) ÷ Sales | 60-65% |
| Occupancy % | Rent+CAM ÷ Sales | 6-10% |

### Profitability Ratios

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Gross Margin | Gross Profit ÷ Revenue | 65-75% |
| Operating Margin | EBITDA ÷ Revenue | 15-20% |
| Net Profit Margin | Net Profit ÷ Revenue | 5-10% |

### Trend Analysis

**Month-over-Month**: Short-term changes.
- Seasonality effects
- Recent initiatives
- Immediate concerns

**Year-over-Year**: Same period comparison.
- Controls for seasonality
- Shows true growth/decline
- Better for trend analysis

## Common P&L Issues

### Revenue Recognition Problems

**Issue**: Revenue not properly recorded.
- Gift cards recorded as revenue when sold (should be when redeemed)
- Catering deposits recorded early
- Timing differences between POS and accounting

**Impact**: Distorts profitability picture.

**Solution**: Proper accounting for timing differences.

### COGS Calculation Errors

**Issue**: Food/beverage cost doesn't reflect actual usage.
- No inventory adjustment
- Purchases recorded but not used
- Missing invoices

**Impact**: Inaccurate food cost percentage.

**Solution**: Regular inventory counts; proper matching.

### Labor Misclassification

**Issue**: Labor costs in wrong categories.
- Manager doing hourly work
- Contractor vs. employee issues
- Benefits not included

**Impact**: Distorted labor cost analysis.

**Solution**: Consistent classification; review regularly.

### Missing Accruals

**Issue**: Expenses not recorded in proper period.
- Utilities received after month-end
- Insurance paid quarterly
- Property tax

**Impact**: Monthly P&L doesn't reflect true costs.

**Solution**: Accrue known recurring expenses.

## Using the P&L for Decisions

### Identifying Profit Leaks

**Process**:
1. Compare each line to target
2. Identify above-target items
3. Quantify potential savings
4. Prioritize action items

**Example**:
- Food cost 33% vs. 30% target
- On $85,000 food sales = $2,550 over
- Annual impact: $30,600
- Warrants immediate investigation

### Evaluating Initiatives

**Before/After Comparison**:
- What was impact of menu change?
- Did marketing investment pay off?
- Is new scheduling approach working?

**Use P&L Data**: Quantify actual results.

### Setting Improvement Targets

**Based on P&L Analysis**:
- Current food cost: 33%
- Target: 30%
- Actions: Portioning, waste reduction, purchasing
- Timeline: 90 days

### Forecasting and Budgeting

**Use P&L History**: Project future performance.
- Revenue trends
- Cost relationships
- Seasonal patterns

## Key Takeaways

The P&L statement is essential for restaurant financial management:

1. **Understand the structure**: Revenue → COGS → Gross Profit → Expenses → Net Profit.

2. **Focus on percentages**: Cost ratios matter more than absolute dollars.

3. **Know your targets**: Food cost, labor cost, prime cost, net margin.

4. **Compare to benchmarks**: Industry standards and your own history.

5. **Identify variances**: Which lines are above/below target?

6. **Take action**: Use P&L insights to drive improvement.

7. **Review regularly**: Monthly at minimum, weekly for key metrics.

8. **Ensure accuracy**: Proper accounting for meaningful analysis.

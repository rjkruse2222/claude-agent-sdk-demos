# Restaurant Debt Management: Strategies for Healthy Leverage

Most restaurants carry some debt—from initial startup loans to equipment financing to lines of credit. Properly managed debt can fuel growth and smooth cash flow, but too much debt or the wrong kind can strangle a business. Understanding how to structure, manage, and if necessary, restructure restaurant debt is essential for long-term financial health.

This guide covers debt management strategies for restaurant operators.

## Understanding Restaurant Debt

### Types of Restaurant Debt

**Term Loans**:
- Fixed amount, fixed term
- Regular principal + interest payments
- Often secured (real estate, equipment)
- Used for startup, expansion, major purchases

**Lines of Credit**:
- Revolving availability
- Draw and repay as needed
- Interest only on amount drawn
- Used for working capital, seasonal needs

**Equipment Financing**:
- Specific to equipment purchase
- Equipment serves as collateral
- Term matches useful life
- May include lease options

**Merchant Cash Advances**:
- Not technically debt (purchase of future sales)
- Very expensive
- Daily repayment from sales
- Use with extreme caution

**Credit Cards**:
- Revolving, high-interest
- Useful for short-term, small purchases
- Dangerous if carried long-term

### Measuring Debt Load

**Key Debt Metrics**:

**Debt-to-Equity Ratio**:
```
Total Debt ÷ Owner's Equity = Debt-to-Equity

Example:
  Total debt: $400,000
  Owner's equity: $200,000
  D/E ratio: 2.0

Interpretation:
  <1.0: Conservative
  1.0-2.0: Moderate
  >2.0: Aggressive/concerning
```

**Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)**:
```
EBITDA ÷ Annual Debt Service = DSCR

Example:
  EBITDA: $180,000
  Annual debt payments: $120,000
  DSCR: 1.5

Interpretation:
  <1.0: Cannot cover debt (crisis)
  1.0-1.25: Tight, at risk
  1.25-1.5: Acceptable
  >1.5: Healthy
```

**Debt-to-Revenue Ratio**:
```
Total Debt ÷ Annual Revenue = Debt/Revenue

Example:
  Total debt: $500,000
  Annual revenue: $1,500,000
  Ratio: 33%

Industry benchmark: Below 50% generally healthy
```

### Good Debt vs. Bad Debt

**Good Debt**:
- Reasonable interest rate
- Funds productive assets
- Terms match asset life
- Cash flow can service comfortably
- Tax benefits from interest deduction

**Bad Debt**:
- High interest rates (MCAs, credit cards)
- Funds operating losses
- Terms too short for purpose
- Strains cash flow
- Overleverages the business

## Debt Structure Optimization

### Matching Term to Purpose

**Principle**: Loan term should match asset useful life.

```
Optimal term matching:

Asset/Purpose          | Useful Life  | Loan Term
-----------------------|--------------|----------
Real estate            | 25-30 years  | 15-25 years
Major equipment        | 7-10 years   | 5-7 years
Technology/POS         | 3-5 years    | 3-4 years
Working capital        | Short cycle  | Line of credit
Seasonal needs         | Months       | Revolving credit
```

### Interest Rate Management

**Fixed vs. Variable**:
- Fixed: Predictable, may be higher initially
- Variable: Lower start, risk of increase
- Consider: Interest rate environment, cash flow stability

**Rate Reduction Strategies**:
- Improve credit profile
- Provide additional collateral
- Refinance when rates fall
- Pay down to improve metrics

### Consolidation Benefits

**When Consolidation Helps**:
```
Before consolidation:
  Equipment loan 1: $50K at 8%, $1,200/mo
  Equipment loan 2: $30K at 9%, $800/mo
  Line of credit: $40K at 10%, interest only $333/mo
  Total monthly: $2,333

After consolidation:
  Single loan: $120K at 7.5%, 7-year term
  Monthly payment: $1,856

Savings: $477/month, $5,724/year
Plus: Simplified management, single payment
```

### Prepayment Strategy

**When to Prepay**:
- Excess cash available
- No better investment use
- Interest rate is high
- Prepayment penalty acceptable

**Prepayment Calculation**:
```
Loan balance: $100,000
Interest rate: 8%
Remaining term: 5 years
Total interest remaining: ~$22,000

Prepayment of $20,000:
  New interest remaining: ~$17,000
  Interest savings: ~$5,000
  ROI on prepayment: 25% (guaranteed)
```

## Managing Debt Service

### Cash Flow Planning

**Build Debt Service Into Budget**:
```
Monthly cash flow with debt service:

Revenue: $120,000
  - COGS (30%): $36,000
  - Labor (30%): $36,000
  - Occupancy (8%): $9,600
  - Operating (10%): $12,000
  - Debt service (8%): $9,600
  - Available for profit/reserves: $16,800 (14%)
```

### Payment Timing

**Optimize Payment Schedule**:
- Align with revenue cycle
- Pay after weekend deposits
- Avoid multiple payments same week
- Build buffer for timing

### Reserve for Debt Service

**Debt Service Reserve**:
```
Monthly debt payments: $10,000
Reserve target: 3 months
Required reserve: $30,000

Benefit: Protection against short-term revenue dips
```

### Covenant Compliance

**Common Loan Covenants**:
- Minimum DSCR (1.25+)
- Maximum debt-to-equity
- Minimum working capital
- Restrictions on additional debt
- Financial reporting requirements

**Monitoring**:
- Track covenant metrics monthly
- Alert lender early if issues
- Negotiate waivers if needed

## When Debt Becomes a Problem

### Warning Signs

**Early Indicators**:
- Borrowing to make debt payments
- Using line of credit at maximum
- Missing covenant requirements
- Paying only minimums on revolving
- Vendor payments slipping

**Crisis Indicators**:
- Cannot make payments
- Default notices received
- Credit shut down
- Considering more expensive debt

### Assessing Debt Crisis

**Questions to Ask**:
- Is this temporary or structural?
- Can operations generate paydown?
- What's the total debt load?
- Which debts are most critical?
- What assets could be liquidated?

### Prioritizing Payments

**When Cash Is Short**:
1. Payroll taxes (personal liability)
2. Secured debt (avoid losing assets)
3. Rent (stay in business)
4. Critical vendors (keep operating)
5. Unsecured debt
6. Credit cards

## Debt Restructuring Options

### Negotiating with Lenders

**What to Request**:
- Interest rate reduction
- Extended term (lower payments)
- Payment deferral
- Principal forgiveness
- Covenant modification

**Approach**:
```
Restructuring discussion points:

1. Be proactive (don't wait for default)
2. Present honest financial picture
3. Show path to recovery
4. Propose specific solution
5. Demonstrate commitment

Example proposal:
"We're requesting a 6-month interest-only period
to allow us to stabilize operations. We project
returning to full payments in July with
accumulated DSCR of 1.4."
```

### Refinancing

**When to Refinance**:
- Rates have dropped
- Credit profile improved
- Better terms available
- Consolidation opportunity

**Process**:
1. Shop multiple lenders
2. Compare total cost (fees + rate)
3. Calculate break-even on costs
4. Ensure no prepayment penalty trap

### Debt Settlement

**Negotiating Payoff**:
```
Scenario: $50,000 unsecured debt, business struggling

Negotiation: Offer $30,000 lump sum settlement
Lender calculation:
  - Collect $30,000 now
  - vs. possibly $0 in bankruptcy
  - vs. years of collection effort

Potential: Accept 60-70% settlement
```

### Professional Help

**When to Engage Professionals**:
- Multiple creditors
- Significant debt load
- Bankruptcy consideration
- Legal actions pending

**Resources**:
- Business turnaround consultants
- Bankruptcy attorneys
- Debt restructuring specialists
- SCORE mentors

## Building Financial Health

### Debt Reduction Plan

**Systematic Paydown**:
```
Debt reduction strategy:

Current debt: $300,000
Target debt: $150,000
Timeline: 3 years

Monthly debt service: $8,000
Additional principal: $2,000
Accelerated monthly: $10,000

Year 1: Reduce to $260,000
Year 2: Reduce to $210,000
Year 3: Reduce to $150,000

Approach: Pay highest rate first (debt avalanche)
```

### Avoiding Future Over-Leverage

**Debt Discipline**:
- Borrow only for productive purposes
- Match terms to asset life
- Maintain DSCR above 1.5
- Keep reserves adequate
- Avoid high-cost debt

## Key Takeaways

Effective debt management supports restaurant financial health:

1. **Know your numbers**: Track D/E ratio, DSCR, and debt/revenue.

2. **Match terms to purpose**: Loan term should align with asset life.

3. **Optimize structure**: Consolidate and refinance when beneficial.

4. **Plan for payments**: Build debt service into cash flow planning.

5. **Monitor covenants**: Stay ahead of potential violations.

6. **Act early on problems**: Proactive communication with lenders.

7. **Avoid bad debt**: High-cost debt rarely solves problems.

8. **Build toward strength**: Systematic paydown improves position.

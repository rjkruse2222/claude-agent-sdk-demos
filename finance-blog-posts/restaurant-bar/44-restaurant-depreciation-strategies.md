# Restaurant Depreciation Strategies: Maximizing Tax Benefits on Equipment and Improvements

Depreciation is one of the most powerful tax deductions for restaurants, allowing you to recover the cost of equipment, furniture, and improvements over time. Strategic use of depreciation rules—including Section 179 expensing and bonus depreciation—can significantly reduce your tax liability. Understanding your options helps you make optimal decisions about timing and method.

This guide covers depreciation strategies for restaurant assets.

## Understanding Depreciation

### What Is Depreciation

**Definition**: A tax deduction that allows businesses to recover the cost of assets over time.

**Why It Exists**: Assets have useful lives beyond one year; depreciation matches expense to benefit period.

**Cash Flow Impact**: Non-cash expense that reduces taxable income.

### Restaurant Assets Subject to Depreciation

**Equipment**:
- Kitchen equipment
- Refrigeration
- POS systems
- Dishwashers
- HVAC systems

**Furniture and Fixtures**:
- Tables and chairs
- Booths
- Bar equipment
- Décor
- Lighting

**Improvements**:
- Leasehold improvements
- Building modifications
- Flooring
- Walls and partitions

**Vehicles**:
- Delivery vehicles
- Catering trucks

## Depreciation Methods

### MACRS Depreciation

**Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System**: Standard method for most business property.

**Recovery Periods for Restaurant Assets**:
| Asset Type | Recovery Period |
|------------|-----------------|
| Kitchen equipment | 5 or 7 years |
| Furniture/fixtures | 7 years |
| Vehicles | 5 years |
| Qualified improvement property | 15 years |
| Building (owned) | 39 years |

### Straight-Line Method

**How It Works**: Equal deduction each year.

**Calculation**:
```
Asset cost: $50,000
Recovery period: 5 years
Annual depreciation: $50,000 ÷ 5 = $10,000
```

**When Required**:
- Alternative minimum tax
- Some asset types
- Election choice

### Accelerated Methods

**200% Declining Balance**: Front-loads depreciation.
- Higher deductions early
- Lower deductions later
- Switches to straight-line when beneficial

**150% Declining Balance**: Moderate acceleration.

**Example Comparison (5-year, $50,000 asset)**:
```
          Straight-Line   200% DB
Year 1:     $10,000       $20,000
Year 2:     $10,000       $12,000
Year 3:     $10,000        $7,200
Year 4:     $10,000        $5,400
Year 5:     $10,000        $5,400
```

## Section 179 Expensing

### What Is Section 179

**Immediate Expensing**: Deduct full cost in year purchased (up to limits).

**2024 Limits**:
- Maximum deduction: $1,160,000
- Phase-out threshold: $2,890,000
- Limited to taxable income

### Qualifying Property

**Eligible Restaurant Assets**:
- Tangible personal property
- Kitchen equipment
- Furniture and fixtures
- POS systems
- Off-the-shelf software
- Some vehicles
- Qualified improvement property

**Not Eligible**:
- Buildings
- Land
- Some leased property

### Section 179 Calculation

**Example**:
```
New kitchen equipment: $75,000
New POS system: $15,000
Furniture: $30,000
Total purchases: $120,000

Section 179 election: Full $120,000
Tax bracket: 32%
Tax savings: $120,000 × 32% = $38,400
```

### Limitations

**Taxable Income Limit**:
- Cannot create or increase loss
- Unused amount carries forward

**Phase-Out**:
- Begins at $2,890,000 of purchases
- Reduced dollar-for-dollar above threshold

### When to Use Section 179

**Beneficial When**:
- Have sufficient taxable income
- Want immediate deduction
- Higher tax bracket this year
- Cash flow from tax savings needed

**Consider Alternatives When**:
- Low income year
- Approaching phase-out
- Future higher brackets expected

## Bonus Depreciation

### Current Bonus Depreciation Rules

**Phase-Down Schedule**:
| Year | Bonus Depreciation |
|------|-------------------|
| 2022 | 100% |
| 2023 | 80% |
| 2024 | 60% |
| 2025 | 40% |
| 2026 | 20% |
| 2027+ | 0% |

### Qualifying Property

**Eligible Assets**:
- New and used property (changed in 2017)
- MACRS property with 20-year or less life
- Qualified improvement property
- Certain film and TV production costs

### Bonus vs. Section 179

**Key Differences**:
| Feature | Section 179 | Bonus Depreciation |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| Dollar limit | $1,160,000 | No limit |
| Income limit | Cannot exceed income | Can create loss |
| Used property | Yes | Yes |
| Election | Elective | Automatic (can opt out) |

### Strategic Use

**Example: $200,000 Equipment Purchase**:
```
Section 179 alone (assuming $150,000 income limit):
  Section 179: $150,000
  Regular depreciation on $50,000: $7,145 (year 1)
  Total year 1 deduction: $157,145

Bonus Depreciation (60%):
  Bonus: $200,000 × 60% = $120,000
  Regular on remaining $80,000: $11,432 (year 1)
  Total year 1 deduction: $131,432

Combined Strategy:
  Section 179: $150,000
  Bonus on $50,000: $30,000
  Regular on $20,000: $2,858
  Total year 1 deduction: $182,858
```

## Restaurant-Specific Considerations

### Qualified Improvement Property (QIP)

**Definition**: Improvements to interior of nonresidential building.

**Recovery Period**: 15 years (corrected by CARES Act).

**Eligibility for Bonus**: Yes, eligible for 60% bonus depreciation.

**Common QIP for Restaurants**:
- Flooring
- Ceilings
- Interior walls
- HVAC upgrades
- Electrical improvements
- Plumbing (interior)

### Leasehold Improvements

**If You Lease Your Space**:
- Improvements may qualify as QIP
- Depreciate over 15 years
- Eligible for bonus depreciation
- Section 179 may apply

**Important**: Tenant improvements are typically the tenant's to depreciate.

### Kitchen Equipment

**Typical Treatment**:
- 5-year or 7-year property
- Qualifies for Section 179
- Qualifies for bonus depreciation

**Common Kitchen Assets**:
```
Equipment               Recovery Period
Ranges/ovens           7 years
Refrigerators          7 years
Dishwashers            7 years
Fryers                 7 years
Prep tables            7 years
Smallwares             Expense or 5 years
```

### POS and Technology

**Computer Equipment**: 5-year property.

**Software**:
- Off-the-shelf: 3 years or Section 179
- Custom: Amortized over 36 months

## Strategic Planning

### Year-End Timing

**Placed in Service Requirement**:
- Equipment must be in use before year-end
- Delivery date matters
- Installation complete

**November/December Purchases**:
- Full year of Section 179/bonus depreciation
- Even if only in use for weeks

### Multi-Year Planning

**Consider Future Brackets**:
```
Scenario: Two $50,000 purchases available
Current year: High income, 32% bracket
Next year: Expected lower income, 24% bracket

Strategy: Maximize deductions this year
- Purchase both items in current year
- Use Section 179 or bonus depreciation
- Save 32% × $100,000 = $32,000
```

### Cash Flow Optimization

**Tax Savings Fund Operations**:
```
Equipment purchase: $100,000
Immediate deduction (Section 179): $100,000
Tax savings at 32%: $32,000
Effective cost: $68,000
```

### Record Keeping

**Documentation Required**:
- Purchase receipts
- Date placed in service
- Asset descriptions
- Depreciation schedules
- Election statements

## Common Mistakes

### Errors to Avoid

**Depreciation Mistakes**:
- Wrong recovery period
- Missing placed-in-service date
- Not claiming bonus depreciation
- Failing to elect Section 179
- Poor records

### Classification Issues

**Repair vs. Improvement**:
```
Repair (deductible immediately):
- Fixing broken equipment
- Routine maintenance
- Minor replacements

Improvement (depreciate):
- New equipment
- Upgrades adding value
- Substantial modifications
```

### Timing Errors

**Placed-in-Service Issues**:
- Equipment delivered but not installed
- Year-end purchases not operational
- Documentation gaps

## Key Takeaways

Strategic depreciation reduces restaurant tax liability significantly:

1. **Know your options**: Regular depreciation, Section 179, bonus depreciation.

2. **Section 179 is powerful**: Up to $1,160,000 immediate deduction.

3. **Bonus depreciation declining**: 60% in 2024, decreasing annually.

4. **QIP is 15 years**: Improvements qualify for bonus depreciation.

5. **Timing matters**: Year-end purchases get full-year benefit.

6. **Plan multi-year**: Consider future tax brackets.

7. **Document carefully**: Placed-in-service dates are critical.

8. **Consult professionals**: Complex rules benefit from expertise.

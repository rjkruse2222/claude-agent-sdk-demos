# Restaurant Accounting 101: Essential Practices Every Owner Should Know

Restaurant accounting is uniquely complex. High transaction volumes, cash handling, tip management, volatile inventory, and multiple revenue streams create challenges that general business accounting doesn't address. Yet solid accounting practices are foundational to profitability—you can't manage what you don't measure accurately.

This comprehensive guide covers the essential accounting practices every restaurant owner needs to understand, whether you handle books yourself or work with professional accountants.

## Why Restaurant Accounting Is Different

Restaurant accounting has unique characteristics that require specialized approaches.

### Unique Challenges

**High Transaction Volume**: Hundreds or thousands of transactions daily create reconciliation complexity.

**Cash Handling**: Physical cash creates security, tracking, and reconciliation requirements.

**Tip Management**: Complex rules for reporting, distributing, and taxing tips.

**Perishable Inventory**: Rapidly turning, difficult-to-value inventory unlike retail.

**Multiple Revenue Streams**: Food, beverage, catering, delivery, gift cards each require tracking.

**Variable Labor**: Hourly workforce with fluctuating schedules and tip income.

**Razor-Thin Margins**: Small percentage errors in any area significantly impact profitability.

### Consequences of Poor Accounting

**Tax Problems**: Incorrect reporting creates liability and penalties.

**Cash Flow Crises**: Poor visibility leads to unexpected shortfalls.

**Theft Undetected**: Without controls, losses go unnoticed.

**Bad Decisions**: Without accurate data, you can't make informed choices.

**Investor/Lender Issues**: Inaccurate records hamper financing.

**Operational Blind Spots**: You don't know what's working and what isn't.

## Choosing an Accounting Method

The foundation of your accounting system is choosing between cash and accrual methods.

### Cash Basis Accounting

**How It Works**: Revenue recorded when received; expenses recorded when paid.

**Advantages**:
- Simpler to implement and understand
- Matches bank account balance more closely
- No accounts receivable or payable tracking required
- Clear picture of cash position

**Disadvantages**:
- Doesn't match revenue to the costs of generating it
- Less accurate picture of profitability
- Can distort results (large bills paid one month affect that month's apparent profit)
- May not satisfy investors or lenders

**Best For**: Small, single-location restaurants with simple operations.

### Accrual Basis Accounting

**How It Works**: Revenue recorded when earned; expenses recorded when incurred (regardless of cash timing).

**Advantages**:
- More accurate picture of profitability
- Matches revenues with related costs
- GAAP compliant (required for audited financials)
- Better for planning and analysis

**Disadvantages**:
- More complex to implement
- Requires tracking receivables and payables
- Doesn't reflect actual cash position
- Requires more accounting expertise

**Best For**: Multi-unit operations, restaurants seeking financing, those wanting detailed analysis.

### IRS Requirements

**Revenue Under $25 Million**: May choose either method.

**Revenue Over $25 Million**: Must use accrual method.

**Consistency**: Once chosen, changing methods requires IRS approval.

## Essential Financial Statements

Three core financial statements provide complete financial picture.

### Profit and Loss Statement (Income Statement)

The P&L shows profitability over a period of time.

**Structure**:
```
Revenue
- Food Sales
- Beverage Sales
- Other Revenue
= Total Revenue

Cost of Goods Sold
- Food Cost
- Beverage Cost
= Gross Profit

Labor Costs
- Hourly Wages
- Management Salaries
- Payroll Taxes
- Benefits
= Total Labor

Prime Cost (COGS + Labor)

Operating Expenses
- Occupancy
- Utilities
- Marketing
- General & Administrative
- Repairs & Maintenance
= Total Operating Expenses

EBITDA (Operating Profit before interest, taxes, depreciation)

Other Expenses
- Depreciation & Amortization
- Interest Expense

Net Income (Profit or Loss)
```

**Key P&L Practices**:
- Review at least monthly
- Compare to budget and prior periods
- Calculate key percentages (food cost %, labor cost %, etc.)
- Investigate significant variances

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet shows financial position at a point in time.

**Structure**:
```
Assets
- Current Assets (cash, inventory, receivables, prepaid expenses)
- Fixed Assets (equipment, furniture, leasehold improvements)
= Total Assets

Liabilities
- Current Liabilities (payables, accrued expenses, short-term debt)
- Long-Term Liabilities (loans, lease obligations)
= Total Liabilities

Equity
- Owner's Equity/Retained Earnings
= Total Equity

Total Liabilities + Equity = Total Assets
```

**Key Balance Sheet Metrics**:
- Current ratio (current assets ÷ current liabilities)
- Working capital (current assets - current liabilities)
- Debt-to-equity ratio

### Cash Flow Statement

Shows cash movement during a period.

**Sections**:
- Operating Activities: Cash from operations
- Investing Activities: Equipment purchases, asset sales
- Financing Activities: Loans, owner investments/distributions

**Importance**: Profitable restaurants can still fail from cash flow problems. Track carefully.

## Daily, Weekly, and Monthly Procedures

Consistent procedures ensure accurate, timely financial information.

### Daily Procedures

**End of Day**:
- Close out POS system and verify totals
- Count and reconcile cash drawers
- Prepare bank deposit
- Record daily sales summary
- Review voids, comps, and discounts

**Daily Sales Report (Flash Report)**:
- Total sales by category
- Guest count and average check
- Labor hours and cost
- Key operational metrics

### Weekly Procedures

**Monday (or your week start)**:
- Reconcile prior week's bank deposits
- Review accounts payable and payment schedule
- Calculate preliminary food cost and labor cost percentages
- Process payroll or prepare for payroll run

**Throughout Week**:
- Enter invoices as received
- Post daily sales to accounting system
- Monitor cash position

**End of Week**:
- Complete inventory count (if weekly)
- Calculate actual food cost
- Reconcile tips

### Monthly Procedures

**First Week of Month**:
- Complete prior month inventory count
- Finalize prior month revenue and expense posting
- Reconcile all bank and credit card accounts
- Process any accrual entries
- Close prior month in accounting system

**Monthly Review**:
- Generate P&L statement
- Compare actual to budget and prior year
- Calculate all key metrics
- Investigate significant variances
- Review balance sheet and cash position

## Managing Cash and Revenue

Cash management is critical in an industry with significant cash transactions.

### Cash Handling Controls

**Separation of Duties**: Different people should count cash, make deposits, and reconcile.

**Daily Deposits**: Deposit all cash receipts daily.

**Limited Access**: Restrict safe access to authorized personnel.

**Documented Procedures**: Written procedures for opening, closing, and cash handling.

**Surprise Counts**: Unannounced cash counts deter theft.

### POS Revenue Tracking

**Daily Close-Out**: Batch and close POS system daily.

**Reconciliation**: Match POS totals to actual cash and credit card receipts.

**Void and Comp Monitoring**: Track and review all voids, comps, and discounts.

**Report Analysis**: Review sales reports for anomalies.

### Credit Card Processing

**Daily Batching**: Close credit card batches daily.

**Reconciliation**: Match credit card deposits to POS reports.

**Fee Monitoring**: Verify processing fees match contracted rates.

**Chargeback Handling**: Process and dispute chargebacks appropriately.

### Gift Card Accounting

**Liability Recognition**: Gift card sales are liability until redeemed.

**Redemption Tracking**: Record revenue when gift cards are used.

**Breakage**: Account for unredeemed balances appropriately.

## Inventory Accounting

Accurate inventory accounting is essential for calculating food cost.

### Inventory Valuation Methods

**FIFO (First In, First Out)**: Assumes oldest inventory used first. Most appropriate for perishable food.

**Weighted Average**: Averages cost of all units available. Simpler but less precise.

**Actual Cost**: Track actual cost paid for each item. Most accurate but most complex.

### Calculating Cost of Goods Sold

**Formula**:
Beginning Inventory + Purchases - Ending Inventory = Cost of Goods Sold

**Example**:
- Beginning Inventory: $15,000
- Purchases: $45,000
- Ending Inventory: $12,000
- COGS = $15,000 + $45,000 - $12,000 = $48,000

### Inventory Counting

**Frequency**: Count at least monthly; weekly is better for control.

**Consistency**: Count at the same time (end of business, before deliveries).

**Accuracy**: Use consistent procedures and verify counts.

**Valuation**: Use current invoice prices for valuation.

### Inventory Technology

Inventory management platforms streamline counting and valuation:

**Mobile Counting**: Count using smartphone or tablet apps.

**Barcode Scanning**: Faster, more accurate counts.

**Automatic Valuation**: Apply current vendor pricing automatically.

**Integration**: Connect to accounting system and POS for automated cost calculation.

## Payroll and Tip Accounting

Restaurant payroll has unique complexity from tips and variable schedules.

### Wage Requirements

**Minimum Wage**: Know your federal, state, and local requirements.

**Tip Credit**: Understand rules if paying below minimum wage to tipped employees.

**Overtime**: Proper calculation for non-exempt employees.

**Break Requirements**: Meal and rest break rules vary by jurisdiction.

### Tip Reporting and Taxes

**Employee Responsibility**: Employees must report all tips.

**Employer Responsibility**: Report tips; withhold taxes; pay FICA on tips.

**Minimum Tip Amount**: 8% of gross receipts is IRS minimum.

**FICA Tip Credit**: Potential credit for FICA taxes paid on tips above minimum wage.

### Tip Pooling and Distribution

**Legal Requirements**: Rules vary by jurisdiction; some prohibit including management.

**Documentation**: Clear policies and accurate records required.

**Distribution Methods**: Track and document all tip distributions.

### Payroll Technology

Modern payroll platforms handle restaurant-specific complexity:

**Time Tracking Integration**: Direct connection from time clocks.

**Tip Tracking**: Manage tip reporting and distribution.

**Multi-Rate Pay**: Handle employees in multiple positions at different rates.

**Compliance**: Automatic application of wage and hour rules.

## Working with Accounting Professionals

Most restaurants benefit from professional accounting support.

### Types of Support

**Bookkeeper**: Day-to-day transaction recording, reconciliation, basic reporting.

**Accountant**: Financial statement preparation, analysis, management reporting.

**CPA**: Tax preparation and planning, audit support, strategic advice.

**Controller (Fractional)**: High-level oversight, systems design, management support.

### Finding the Right Support

**Restaurant Experience**: General accountants may not understand industry nuances.

**Technology Proficiency**: Comfort with restaurant management platforms.

**Communication Style**: Regular, clear communication about your finances.

**Appropriate Level**: Match support level to your size and complexity.

### Working Effectively Together

**Provide Timely Information**: Get invoices, statements, and data to them on schedule.

**Ask Questions**: Understand what reports mean and how to use them.

**Regular Meetings**: Monthly or quarterly reviews of financial performance.

**Strategic Input**: Use them as business advisors, not just number-crunchers.

## Key Takeaways

Strong accounting practices are essential for restaurant success:

1. **Choose your method carefully**: Cash vs. accrual has significant implications.

2. **Know your statements**: Understand P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow.

3. **Design a useful chart of accounts**: Detailed categorization enables meaningful analysis.

4. **Follow consistent procedures**: Daily, weekly, and monthly routines ensure accuracy.

5. **Control cash carefully**: High cash volume requires strong controls.

6. **Track inventory accurately**: Proper inventory accounting is essential for food cost.

7. **Handle payroll correctly**: Tips and variable schedules create complexity.

8. **Get professional help**: Restaurant-experienced accountants add significant value.

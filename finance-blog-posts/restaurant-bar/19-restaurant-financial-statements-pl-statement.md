# Restaurant Financial Statements: How to Read and Use Your P&L Statement

The Profit and Loss statement—also called the income statement—is the most important financial report for restaurant operators. It tells you whether your restaurant is making or losing money and, more importantly, why. Yet many restaurant owners receive their P&L and file it away without truly understanding what it reveals about their business.

This guide teaches you how to read, analyze, and use your P&L statement to make better business decisions.

## Understanding the P&L Statement Structure

The P&L shows your restaurant's financial performance over a period of time—typically monthly, quarterly, or annually.

### Basic P&L Structure

```
Revenue (Sales)
- Cost of Goods Sold
= Gross Profit

- Labor Costs
= Prime Cost Profit

- Operating Expenses
= Operating Profit (EBITDA)

- Depreciation and Amortization
- Interest Expense
= Pre-Tax Profit

- Income Taxes
= Net Profit (Net Income)
```

### Reading Direction

P&L statements read from top to bottom:
- **Top**: All revenue coming in
- **Middle**: Costs deducted in logical order
- **Bottom**: Remaining profit (or loss)

Each subtraction shows another layer of costs, helping identify where money goes.

### Key Profit Lines

**Gross Profit**: Revenue minus COGS; shows margin after product costs.

**Prime Cost Profit**: After food and labor; your two largest controllable costs.

**Operating Profit (EBITDA)**: Profit from operations before financing and accounting adjustments.

**Net Profit**: Final bottom line after all expenses.

## Key Line Items Explained

Understanding each P&L component enables meaningful analysis.

### Revenue Section

**Total Revenue**: All sales and other income.

**Food Sales**: Revenue from food items sold.

**Beverage Sales**: Revenue from drinks; often broken into non-alcoholic, beer, wine, liquor.

**Other Revenue**: Catering, private events, merchandise, fees.

**Contra Revenue**: Deductions from gross sales (discounts, comps, employee meals).

Net Sales = Gross Sales - Contra Revenue

### Cost of Goods Sold

**Food Cost**: Cost of ingredients used to produce food sold.

**Beverage Cost**: Cost of drinks sold, typically tracked separately by category.

**Paper Goods**: Disposable items (containers, napkins) included with product.

COGS calculation (accrual method):
Beginning Inventory + Purchases - Ending Inventory = COGS

### Labor Costs

**Hourly Wages**: Pay to non-exempt employees by the hour.

**Salaries**: Fixed pay to exempt employees (managers).

**Payroll Taxes**: Employer portion of FICA, unemployment taxes.

**Benefits**: Health insurance, retirement, PTO, and other benefits.

**Workers' Compensation**: Required insurance for workplace injuries.

Total Labor = Wages + Salaries + Taxes + Benefits + Workers' Comp

### Operating Expenses

**Occupancy**: Rent, CAM, property tax, property insurance.

**Utilities**: Electric, gas, water, trash, phone/internet.

**Marketing**: Advertising, promotions, loyalty programs.

**Direct Operating**: Supplies, smallwares, linens, uniforms.

**General & Administrative**: Accounting, legal, office, bank fees.

**Repairs & Maintenance**: Building and equipment upkeep.

**Technology**: POS, software subscriptions, IT support.

### Below-the-Line Items

**Depreciation**: Non-cash expense reflecting asset value decline.

**Amortization**: Similar to depreciation for intangible assets.

**Interest Expense**: Cost of borrowed money.

**Income Taxes**: Federal, state, local income taxes.

## Calculating Essential Metrics

Convert dollar amounts to percentages for meaningful analysis.

### Percentage Calculations

For each line item:
**Percentage = Line Item Amount ÷ Total Revenue × 100**

Example: $45,000 food cost on $150,000 revenue
Food Cost % = $45,000 ÷ $150,000 × 100 = 30%

### Key Metrics to Calculate

**Food Cost %** = Food COGS ÷ Food Sales × 100
Target: 28-35% depending on concept

**Beverage Cost %** = Beverage COGS ÷ Beverage Sales × 100
Target: 18-24% for combined beverages

**Total COGS %** = Total COGS ÷ Total Revenue × 100
Target: 25-35%

**Labor Cost %** = Total Labor ÷ Total Revenue × 100
Target: 25-35% depending on service style

**Prime Cost %** = (COGS + Labor) ÷ Total Revenue × 100
Target: 55-65%

**Occupancy %** = Occupancy Costs ÷ Total Revenue × 100
Target: 6-10%

**Net Profit %** = Net Income ÷ Total Revenue × 100
Target: 3-10% depending on concept

### Contribution Margin

Shows profit after variable costs:
**Contribution Margin = Revenue - Variable Costs**

This indicates how much each dollar of sales contributes to covering fixed costs and profit.

## Analyzing Your P&L Statement

Move beyond looking at numbers to understanding what they mean.

### Trend Analysis

Compare current period to:
- Same period last year (seasonal comparison)
- Prior month (recent trend)
- Year-to-date vs. prior year-to-date
- Rolling 12-month average

Look for:
- Improving or declining trends
- Unusual spikes or drops
- Seasonal patterns
- Acceleration or deceleration of trends

### Variance Analysis

Compare actual results to:
- Budget (planned vs. actual)
- Prior period
- Industry benchmarks

For significant variances, ask:
- What specifically caused this variance?
- Is it a one-time issue or ongoing problem?
- What action is needed?

### Common Sense Checks

**Revenue Reasonableness**: Does revenue match guest counts × average check?

**COGS to Inventory**: Does food cost align with inventory changes and purchases?

**Labor to Hours**: Does labor cost match scheduled hours × wage rates?

**Expense Completeness**: Are all expenses captured, or are some missing?

### Multi-Unit Comparison

If operating multiple locations:
- Compare same metrics across units
- Identify best and worst performers by category
- Investigate significant differences
- Share best practices from top performers

## Benchmarking Against Industry Standards

Compare your metrics to industry benchmarks for your restaurant type.

### Typical P&L Benchmarks by Restaurant Type

| Metric | QSR | Fast Casual | Casual | Fine Dining |
|--------|-----|-------------|--------|-------------|
| Food Cost | 25-30% | 28-32% | 28-35% | 30-40% |
| Beverage Cost | 20-25% | 20-25% | 18-24% | 22-30% |
| Labor Cost | 25-30% | 28-32% | 30-35% | 35-40% |
| Prime Cost | 55-60% | 58-62% | 60-65% | 62-68% |
| Occupancy | 6-10% | 6-10% | 6-10% | 8-12% |
| Net Profit | 6-9% | 5-8% | 3-6% | 2-6% |

### Interpreting Benchmark Comparisons

**At or Below Benchmark**: Performing well in this area.

**Slightly Above Benchmark**: Monitor and look for improvement opportunities.

**Significantly Above Benchmark**: Urgent attention needed; likely impacting profitability.

### Benchmark Limitations

Remember that benchmarks are averages:
- Your concept may differ from "average"
- Local conditions affect appropriate targets
- Your own history may be more relevant
- Use as guidelines, not absolute rules

## Using P&L Insights for Decision-Making

P&L analysis should drive specific actions.

### Revenue Insights

**Declining Revenue**: Is it traffic or average check? Which dayparts? Which categories?

**Actions**: Marketing, menu changes, service improvements, pricing adjustments.

**Revenue Mix Shifts**: Are high-margin categories growing or shrinking?

**Actions**: Menu placement, server training, promotional focus.

### COGS Insights

**Rising Food Cost %**: Is it prices, portions, waste, or theft?

**Actions**: Supplier negotiation, portion control, waste tracking, menu engineering.

**Beverage Cost Variance**: Which categories are off target?

**Actions**: Pour control, inventory management, pricing review.

### Labor Insights

**High Labor Cost %**: Is it scheduling, productivity, wages, or overtime?

**Actions**: Scheduling optimization, cross-training, productivity improvement.

**Labor Category Analysis**: Where specifically are costs high?

**Actions**: Targeted adjustments to specific positions or shifts.

### Operating Expense Insights

**Rising Utilities**: Usage or rates?

**Actions**: Energy efficiency, rate negotiation, usage management.

**Marketing ROI**: Is marketing spend generating returns?

**Actions**: Redirect spending to effective channels.

## Common P&L Problems and Solutions

### Problem: Food Cost Creep

**Symptoms**: Gradual increase over several months.

**Investigation**: Compare to theoretical food cost; analyze by category.

**Solutions**: Portion control, supplier review, menu engineering, waste reduction.

### Problem: Labor Spikes

**Symptoms**: Labor cost jumps in specific periods.

**Investigation**: Review schedules vs. sales; check for overtime.

**Solutions**: Better forecasting, scheduling discipline, overtime management.

### Problem: Unexplained Low Profit

**Symptoms**: Revenue okay, costs seem okay, but profit is low.

**Investigation**: Check for missing expenses, verify accruals, review all categories.

**Solutions**: Complete expense capture, proper accrual accounting.

### Problem: Inconsistent Results

**Symptoms**: High variability month to month.

**Investigation**: Timing issues? Accounting errors? Operational inconsistency?

**Solutions**: Improve accrual accounting, consistent procedures, better forecasting.

## Key Takeaways

Your P&L statement is essential for understanding and improving restaurant performance:

1. **Understand the structure**: Revenue at top, costs subtracted, profit at bottom.

2. **Know key line items**: COGS, labor, and operating expenses drive profitability.

3. **Calculate percentages**: Convert dollars to percentages for meaningful analysis.

4. **Analyze trends and variances**: Compare to budget, prior periods, and benchmarks.

5. **Benchmark appropriately**: Use industry standards as guidelines, adjusted for your concept.

6. **Drive decisions**: P&L insights should lead to specific actions.

7. **Investigate problems**: Dig into variances to understand root causes.

8. **Review regularly**: Monthly P&L review should be a standard practice.

# Draft Beer System Profitability: Maximizing Tap Revenue

Draft beer can be one of the most profitable items on your menu—when managed correctly. With proper pour control, pricing strategy, and maintenance, draft beer margins exceed bottled beer and approach cocktail profitability. However, waste from foam, spillage, and line maintenance can erode profits if not carefully monitored.

This guide covers financial optimization of draft beer systems.

## Draft Beer Economics

### Why Draft Beer Is Profitable

**Higher Margins**: Draft typically outperforms bottles/cans.
- Bottled beer cost: 25-30%
- Draft beer cost: 20-25% (well-managed)
- Volume purchasing advantages

**Customer Preference**: Many prefer draft.
- Perceived freshness
- Presentation value
- Social experience

**Incremental Efficiency**:
- No bottle inventory
- Less glass recycling
- Faster service

### Understanding Draft Costs

**Per-Keg Calculation**:
```
Half-barrel keg: 15.5 gallons = 1,984 ounces
Standard pour: 16 oz
Maximum pours per keg: 124 pints

Keg cost: $180
Cost per pint (theoretical): $1.45
```

**But Reality Differs**: Yield is never 100%.

### Yield Considerations

**Actual Yield**: 80-90% of theoretical.

**Losses Include**:
- Foam during pour
- Line waste
- Over-pouring
- Spillage
- Sampling

**Adjusted Calculation**:
```
124 theoretical pints × 85% yield = 105 actual pints
$180 keg cost ÷ 105 pints = $1.71 actual cost per pint
```

## System Costs and Setup

### Initial Investment

**Equipment Costs**:
- Draft tower/tap system: $500-2,000 per tap
- Kegerator/cooler: $1,000-10,000
- Lines and fittings: $100-300 per tap
- Glycol system (long draw): $2,000-5,000

**Number of Taps**: Consider carefully.
- Too few: Limited selection
- Too many: Inventory burden
- Sweet spot varies by concept

### Ongoing Costs

**Equipment Maintenance**:
- Line cleaning supplies
- Replacement parts
- Professional maintenance

**CO2/Nitrogen**:
- Gas costs for dispensing
- Tank rental or purchase
- Varies with volume

**Line Cleaning**:
- Every 2 weeks recommended
- $10-25 per line if outsourced
- DIY requires equipment and time

### Break-Even Analysis

**Example System ROI**:
```
System investment: $8,000 (4-tap system)
Additional profit per pint (vs. bottle): $1.50
Pints needed to break even: 5,333
At 100 draft pints/week: 53 weeks to break even
```

## Pricing Strategies

### Standard Pour Sizes

**Common Options**:
- 10 oz: Taster/small
- 16 oz: Standard pint
- 20 oz: Imperial pint
- 23 oz: Large/pitcher size

**Glassware Matters**: Actual vs. perceived size.

### Pricing Methods

**Cost-Plus**:
```
Target pour cost: 22%
Cost per pint: $1.71
Price = $1.71 ÷ 0.22 = $7.77 (round to $7.50 or $8.00)
```

**Market-Based**: What similar establishments charge.

**Tiered Pricing**:
| Beer Type | Cost/Pint | Pour Cost Target | Price |
|-----------|-----------|------------------|-------|
| Domestic | $0.90 | 15% | $6 |
| Craft | $1.70 | 22% | $8 |
| Import | $1.50 | 20% | $7.50 |
| Premium Craft | $2.50 | 25% | $10 |

### Happy Hour Strategy

**Reduced Price Considerations**:
- Still must cover cost
- Minimum margin maintained
- Volume increase required

**Example**:
```
Regular price: $8 (22% cost)
Happy hour: $5 (35% cost)
Margin reduced but volume may increase
```

### Size Pricing Psychology

**Upselling Opportunity**:
```
16 oz: $7
20 oz: $8.50
23 oz: $9.50

Incremental cost of 4 extra oz: $0.40
Incremental revenue: $1.50
Margin improvement on larger size
```

## Pour Control and Yield

### The Yield Equation

**Theoretical Yield**: What keg should produce.

**Actual Yield**: What you actually sell.

**Variance**: Difference = waste + theft + over-pour.

### Calculating Your Yield

**Method**:
```
Step 1: Track kegs used (by brand)
Step 2: Track pints sold (from POS)
Step 3: Calculate yield

Example:
Kegs used: 2 (248 theoretical pints)
Pints sold: 200
Yield: 200 ÷ 248 = 80.6%
```

### Pour Standards

**Training Elements**:
- Proper glass angle
- Distance from tap
- Opening and closing technique
- Foam management

**Target Pour**: 3/4" to 1" head.
- Less = over-pour (cost)
- More = customer complaints

### Technology Solutions

**Flow Meters**:
- Track every ounce poured
- By tap and by time
- Identify variance

**Costs**: $200-1,000 per tap.

**Benefits**:
- Accountability
- Variance detection
- Inventory accuracy

### Theft Prevention

**Common Theft Methods**:
- Giving away beer
- Under-ringing
- Selling from keg not in system

**Controls**:
- POS tracking of all pours
- Matching sales to keg usage
- Management oversight
- Flow monitoring

## Waste Management

### Sources of Waste

**Foam (Largest Source)**:
- Improper temperature
- Pressure issues
- Dirty lines
- Pour technique

**Line Waste**:
- Beer in lines between kegs
- Line cleaning
- Keg changes

**Spillage**:
- Dropped glasses
- Over-filling
- Accidents

**Sampling**:
- Customer samples
- Staff sampling
- Quality checks

### Target Waste Levels

**Industry Standard**: 15-20% waste acceptable.

**Well-Managed**: 10-15% waste.

**Excellent**: Under 10% waste.

### Reducing Foam Waste

**Temperature Control**:
- Beer should be 36-38°F
- Lines must be cold (glycol for long runs)
- Glasses should be clean and room temp

**Pressure Settings**:
- Correct CO2 pressure for style
- Balance pressure with line length
- Check gauges regularly

**Glass Preparation**:
- Clean glasses (no residue)
- Room temperature (not frozen)
- Proper rinsing

### Tracking Waste

**Log All Waste**:
- Foam pours
- Spillage
- Samples given
- Line cleaning

**Compare to POS**: Identify unexplained variance.

## Line Maintenance Impact

### Why Line Cleaning Matters

**Dirty Lines Cause**:
- Off-flavors
- Excess foam
- Customer complaints
- Lost sales
- Health issues

**Clean Lines Deliver**:
- Better taste
- Less foam waste
- Happy customers
- Higher margins

### Cleaning Frequency

**Recommendation**: Every 2 weeks minimum.

**High-Volume**: Weekly may be appropriate.

**Components to Clean**:
- Beer lines
- Faucets
- Keg couplers
- Drip trays

### Cleaning Options

**Professional Service**:
- Cost: $10-25 per line
- Consistent quality
- No staff time required
- Typically every 2 weeks

**In-House**:
- Equipment investment: $300-800
- Chemical costs ongoing
- Staff time required
- More control over frequency

### Cost-Benefit of Line Maintenance

**Skipping Cleaning Costs**:
- 5% additional foam waste on $150 keg = $7.50
- Customer complaints and returns
- Reputation damage
- Health risk

**Cleaning Cost**: $15-25 per line every 2 weeks.

**Clear Winner**: Regular cleaning pays for itself.

## Managing Your Draft Program

### Tap Selection

- Customer preferences
- Margin by style
- Distributor relationships
- Storage/rotation

**Recommended Mix**:
- 1-2 domestic macro (high volume)

**Rotation**: First in, first out.

- Pints sold by tap

## Key Takeaways

Maximizing draft beer profitability requires attention to detail:

1. **Understand true yield**: 80-90%, not theoretical 100%.

2. **Price for actual cost**: Include waste in calculations.

3. **Control pours**: Training and technology help.

4. **Clean lines regularly**: Every 2 weeks minimum.

5. **Track yield by tap**: Identify problems quickly.

6. **Manage temperature and pressure**: Reduce foam waste.

7. **Rotate selection strategically**: Balance volume and variety.

8. **Monitor metrics weekly**: Revenue, yield, pour cost.

# Tipped Employee Wage Calculations: Compliance and Best Practices

Calculating wages for tipped employees involves unique complexities—tip credits, minimum wage requirements, overtime on tipped wages, and tip pooling rules. Getting these calculations wrong exposes restaurants to significant liability. Understanding the rules and implementing compliant practices protects both your business and your employees.

This guide explains tipped employee wage calculations and compliance requirements.

## Understanding Tip Credit

### What Is Tip Credit?

Tip credit allows employers to pay tipped employees below the standard minimum wage, with tips making up the difference. The employee's total compensation (cash wage plus tips) must meet or exceed minimum wage.

### Federal Tip Credit Rules

**Federal Minimum Wage**: $7.25/hour

**Federal Tipped Minimum Wage**: $2.13/hour

**Maximum Tip Credit**: $5.12/hour ($7.25 - $2.13)

**Requirement**: Employee must receive at least federal minimum wage when cash wage and tips are combined.

### State Tip Credit Variations

States have varying rules:

**No Tip Credit Allowed** (full minimum wage required):
- California
- Washington
- Oregon
- Alaska
- Montana
- Minnesota
- Nevada (at certain wage levels)

**Reduced Tip Credit** (higher cash minimum than federal):
- Many states require higher cash wages
- Example: New York requires $10.00 cash wage for NYC restaurants

**Federal Rules Apply**:
- Some states follow federal $2.13 tipped minimum

**Always Research Your State**: Rules vary significantly.

### Tip Credit Requirements

**To Take Tip Credit, Employer Must**:

1. **Inform Employees**: Explain tip credit provisions before using.

2. **Pay Cash Wage**: At least the tipped minimum wage.

3. **Allow Tip Retention**: Employee keeps all tips (except valid tip pool).

4. **Ensure Minimum Wage**: Make up difference if tips insufficient.

### Sample Tip Credit Notice

Employers should provide notice covering:
- Cash wage paid
- Tip credit claimed
- Tips must bring total to minimum wage
- All tips are employee's property
- Tip pool details if applicable

## Minimum Wage Compliance

### The Make-Up Pay Requirement

If tips don't bring total to minimum wage, employer must make up the difference.

**Example (Using Federal Rates)**:
```
Cash wage: $2.13/hour × 40 hours = $85.20
Tips earned: $150.00
Total compensation: $235.20
Minimum required: $7.25 × 40 = $290.00
Make-up pay required: $54.80
```

### When to Calculate

**Options**:
- Per pay period (most common)
- Per workweek (some states require)

**Recommendation**: Calculate per workweek for safety.

### Calculation Methods

**Workweek Calculation**:
```
Step 1: Calculate total hours worked
Step 2: Calculate cash wages paid
Step 3: Calculate tips received
Step 4: Add cash wages + tips = total compensation
Step 5: Calculate minimum wage requirement (hours × min wage)
Step 6: If total compensation < minimum requirement, pay difference
```

**Example**:
```
Server works 35 hours
Cash wage: $2.13 × 35 = $74.55
Tips: $220.00
Total: $294.55
Minimum requirement: $7.25 × 35 = $253.75
Result: $294.55 > $253.75, no make-up pay needed
```

### State Minimum Wage

**When State Exceeds Federal**: Use higher state minimum.

**Example** (California):
- No tip credit allowed
- Minimum wage applies (varies by employer size)
- Tips are in addition to minimum wage

## Overtime Calculations

### Overtime for Tipped Employees

Overtime calculations for tipped employees are complex because the regular rate includes tips.

### The Regular Rate

**Regular Rate** = (Cash wages + Tips) ÷ Hours worked

**Overtime Rate** = 1.5 × Regular Rate

### Overtime Calculation Methods

**Full Overtime Method**:
Calculate actual regular rate including tips; pay 1.5× that rate.

```
Week: 50 hours
Cash wage: $2.13/hour
Tips: $400

Regular rate: ($2.13 × 50 + $400) ÷ 50 = $10.13/hour
Overtime premium: $10.13 × 0.5 × 10 hours = $50.65
Total pay: $106.50 (cash wages) + $400 (tips) + $50.65 (OT premium) = $557.15
```

**Tip Credit Overtime Method** (More Common):
Pay cash overtime at 1.5× minimum wage minus tip credit.

```
Week: 50 hours
Cash wage: $2.13/hour
Tips: $400
Federal minimum: $7.25

Overtime rate: ($7.25 × 1.5) - $5.12 tip credit = $5.75/hour
Regular hours: $2.13 × 40 = $85.20
Overtime hours: $5.75 × 10 = $57.50
Total cash: $142.70
Tips: $400
Total compensation: $542.70
```

### State Overtime Rules

Some states have additional requirements:
- Daily overtime (California: over 8 hours/day)
- Different overtime thresholds
- Higher minimum wage affects calculation

## Tip Pooling Rules

### What Is Tip Pooling?

Sharing tips among employees who contribute to customer service.

### Traditional Tip Pool Rules

**Eligible Participants** (traditionally):
- Servers
- Bussers
- Hosts
- Bartenders
- Other customer-facing employees

**Ineligible Participants** (traditionally):
- Managers
- Back-of-house (when tip credit taken)

### Recent Rule Changes

**When Employer Does NOT Take Tip Credit**:
- Back-of-house can participate in pool
- Managers and supervisors still excluded

**When Employer Takes Tip Credit**:
- Only traditionally tipped employees can participate
- Back-of-house excluded
- Servers cannot be required to share with kitchen

### Tip Pool Requirements

**Must Be**:
- Customary and reasonable
- Distributed fairly
- Not include managers/supervisors

**Prohibited**:
- Employer cannot retain any tips
- Cannot require tips go to non-eligible employees
- Cannot use tips to offset wage obligations

### Sample Tip Pool Distribution

```
SHIFT TIP POOL
Total tips collected: $800

Distribution (by points):
Server (6 points each):
  Server 1: 6 points
  Server 2: 6 points
  Server 3: 6 points

Busser (3 points each):
  Busser 1: 3 points
  Busser 2: 3 points

Host (2 points):
  Host 1: 2 points

Total points: 26

Per point value: $800 ÷ 26 = $30.77

Distribution:
  Each server: $184.62
  Each busser: $92.31
  Host: $61.54
  Total: $800.00
```

## Record-Keeping Requirements

### Required Records

**For Each Tipped Employee**:
- Name, address, social security number
- Cash wage paid
- Weekly tip credit claimed
- Tips reported by employee
- Total hours worked
- Regular and overtime hours
- Total wages paid

### Tip Reporting by Employees

**Employee Responsibility**:
- Report cash tips of $20+ per month
- Report by 10th of following month

**Employer Responsibility**:
- Provide reporting method
- Include reported tips in wages
- Withhold taxes on tips

### Documentation Best Practices

**Maintain**:
- Tip credit notices (signed)
- Tip pool policies
- Tip reporting forms
- Payroll records
- Time records

**Retention**: Minimum 3 years (some states require longer).

## Common Compliance Issues

### Failing to Make Up Minimum Wage

**Problem**: Not calculating tip shortfall.

**Risk**: Back wages, liquidated damages, penalties.

**Solution**: Calculate every pay period; automate if possible.

### Improper Overtime Calculation

**Problem**: Not including tips in regular rate.

**Risk**: Underpayment of overtime.

**Solution**: Use proper formula; consult with payroll specialist.

### Invalid Tip Pools

**Problem**: Including ineligible employees.

**Risk**: Employee lawsuits, DOL violations.

**Solution**: Review pool composition; follow current rules.

### No Tip Credit Notice

**Problem**: Taking credit without informing employees.

**Risk**: Cannot claim tip credit; owe back wages.

**Solution**: Provide written notice; keep signed copies.

### Deductions from Tips

**Problem**: Deducting for walkouts, breakage, or credit card fees.

**Risk**: Potential FLSA violations in some circumstances.

**Solution**: Understand rules; consult counsel before deductions.

### Managers Taking Tips

**Problem**: Including supervisors in pool or taking tips.

**Risk**: Significant liability; employee lawsuits.

**Solution**: Exclude managers; define "manager" properly.

## Key Takeaways

Proper tipped employee wage calculations protect your business:

1. **Know your state rules**: Tip credit varies significantly by state.

2. **Provide written notice**: Required before taking tip credit.

3. **Calculate minimum wage compliance**: Every pay period.

4. **Pay overtime correctly**: Include tips in regular rate calculation.

5. **Structure tip pools properly**: Only eligible employees.

6. **Exclude managers**: From tip pools and tip retention.

7. **Maintain records**: Documentation is your defense.

8. **Audit regularly**: Catch issues before they become lawsuits.

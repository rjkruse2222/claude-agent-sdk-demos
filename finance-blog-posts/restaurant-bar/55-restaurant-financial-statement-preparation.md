# Restaurant Financial Statement Preparation: Creating Accurate Reports

Accurate financial statements are essential for managing your restaurant, securing financing, and meeting compliance requirements. Whether preparing statements internally or working with an accountant, understanding the preparation process ensures reliable data for decision-making.

This guide covers financial statement preparation for restaurant operations.

## Financial Statement Overview

### The Three Primary Statements

**Profit and Loss (Income Statement)**:
- Shows profitability over a period
- Revenue minus expenses equals net income
- Most frequently reviewed by operators

**Balance Sheet**:
- Shows financial position at a point in time
- Assets, liabilities, and equity
- Snapshot of what you own and owe

**Cash Flow Statement**:
- Shows cash movement over a period
- Operating, investing, financing activities
- Explains changes in cash position

### Statement Relationships

**How They Connect**:
- Net income (P&L) flows to retained earnings (Balance Sheet)
- Net income is starting point for cash flow statement
- Ending cash (Cash Flow) matches cash on balance sheet

### Reporting Frequency

**Monthly**: Most common for restaurant management.
- Timely information
- Monthly P&L most critical
- Balance sheet may be quarterly

**Quarterly**: For investors, lenders.
- Required by some loan covenants
- More formal preparation

**Annually**: For taxes, comprehensive review.
- May require CPA preparation
- Year-end adjustments

## The Monthly Close Process

### Why a Formal Close Matters

**Accuracy**: Ensure all transactions captured.

**Consistency**: Same process each month.

**Timeliness**: Financial information when needed.

**Comparability**: Apples-to-apples across periods.

### Close Calendar

**Week 1 (Days 1-5)**:
- Finalize prior month sales
- Receive and enter final invoices
- Process payroll (if bi-weekly)
- Reconcile bank accounts

**Week 2 (Days 6-10)**:
- Complete inventory count and valuation
- Record accruals and adjustments
- Review and correct coding
- Prepare preliminary statements

**Mid-Month (Days 10-15)**:
- Management review of statements
- Finalize adjustments
- Produce final statements
- Distribute reports

### Close Checklist

```
MONTHLY CLOSE CHECKLIST

Revenue:
□ All sales recorded through month-end
□ Credit card settlements reconciled
□ Third-party delivery revenue recorded
□ Gift card transactions properly recorded

Expenses:
□ All vendor invoices entered
□ Payroll recorded (including accrual if needed)
□ Rent and recurring expenses recorded
□ Estimated expenses accrued

Inventory:
□ Physical count completed
□ Inventory valued correctly
□ Cost of goods sold calculated

Reconciliations:
□ Bank accounts reconciled
□ Credit card accounts reconciled
□ Accounts receivable reviewed
□ Accounts payable reviewed

Adjustments:
□ Depreciation recorded
□ Prepaid expenses amortized
□ Accrued expenses recorded
□ Prior period corrections made

Review:
□ Trial balance reviewed
□ P&L compared to budget and prior year
□ Balance sheet accounts verified
□ Final statements produced
```

## Preparing the Profit and Loss Statement

### Revenue Recording

**Sales from POS**:
- Import or enter daily sales
- Separate food, beverage, other
- Include discounts and comps as contra-revenue

**Credit Card Processing**:
- Gross sales recorded as revenue
- Processing fees as expense
- Net deposits reconciled to bank

**Other Revenue**:
- Catering income
- Private events
- Gift card redemptions (from liability)

### Cost of Goods Sold

**Calculation Method**:
```
Beginning Inventory
+ Purchases
- Ending Inventory
= Cost of Goods Sold
```

**Inventory Count**:
- Physical count end of month
- Value at cost (most recent or average)
- Separate food and beverage

**Purchases**:
- All invoices for food and beverage
- Properly coded
- In correct period

### Labor Costs

**Components**:
- Salaries and wages (from payroll)
- Payroll taxes (employer portion)
- Benefits (health, retirement, etc.)

**Pay Period Alignment**:
- Accrue wages for days earned but not paid
- Ensures expense matches period worked

**Example**:
- Month ends Wednesday
- Payday is Friday for prior week
- Accrue Monday-Wednesday wages

### Operating Expenses

**Recording Principles**:
- Match expense to period incurred
- Accrue known expenses without invoices
- Allocate prepaid expenses

**Common Accruals**:
- Utilities (often billed after month-end)
- Property tax (annual bill, monthly accrual)
- Insurance (if paid annually)

**Prepaid Amortization**:
- Insurance paid annually: 1/12 per month
- Annual licenses: 1/12 per month
- Deposits: Remain on balance sheet

## Preparing the Balance Sheet

### Asset Accounts

**Cash**:
- Reconciled bank balances
- Petty cash counted
- Deposits in transit

**Accounts Receivable**:
- Open catering invoices
- House accounts outstanding
- Third-party delivery pending
- Less: allowance for doubtful accounts

**Inventory**:
- Per physical count
- Valued consistently
- Food, beverage, supplies

**Fixed Assets**:
- All assets at cost
- Additions recorded
- Disposals removed
- Accumulated depreciation updated

### Liability Accounts

**Accounts Payable**:
- All unpaid vendor invoices
- Reconcile to vendor statements
- Age and review for accuracy

**Accrued Liabilities**:
- Wages payable
- Payroll taxes payable
- Sales tax payable
- Other accrued expenses

**Gift Card Liability**:
- Total unredeemed gift cards
- Reduced as cards redeemed

**Debt**:
- Current portion vs. long-term
- Reconcile to loan statements
- Proper classification

### Equity Section

**Changes in Equity**:
- Prior period retained earnings
- Add: Net income for period
- Less: Distributions/draws
- Equals: Ending retained earnings

## Preparing the Cash Flow Statement

### Operating Activities

**Start with Net Income**

**Add Back Non-Cash Expenses**:
- Depreciation
- Amortization

**Adjust for Working Capital Changes**:
- Decrease in receivables = cash inflow
- Increase in inventory = cash outflow
- Increase in payables = cash inflow

### Investing Activities

**Capital Expenditures**:
- Equipment purchases
- Improvements
- Technology

**Asset Sales**:
- Proceeds from selling equipment
- Sale of investments

### Financing Activities

**Loan Activity**:
- Proceeds from borrowing
- Principal payments

**Owner Activity**:
- Capital contributions
- Distributions/draws

### Sample Cash Flow

```
CASH FLOW STATEMENT
Month Ending October 31

Operating Activities:
  Net Income                      $12,472
  Add: Depreciation                $2,200
  Changes in Working Capital:
    Accounts Receivable              ($800)
    Inventory                        ($500)
    Prepaid Expenses                  $200
    Accounts Payable                 $1,500
    Accrued Liabilities               $600
  Net Cash from Operations        $15,672

Investing Activities:
  Equipment Purchases             ($5,000)
  Net Cash from Investing         ($5,000)

Financing Activities:
  Loan Principal Payments         ($2,000)
  Owner Draws                     ($4,000)
  Net Cash from Financing         ($6,000)

Net Change in Cash                 $4,672
Beginning Cash                    $30,328
Ending Cash                       $35,000
```

## Ensuring Accuracy

### Reconciliation Procedures

**Bank Reconciliation**: Every account, every month.
- Statement balance to book balance
- Identify and research differences
- Clear old outstanding items

**Credit Card Reconciliation**:
- Processor statements to deposits
- Processing fees verified

**Vendor Statement Reconciliation**:
- Major vendors monthly
- All vendors quarterly
- Identify missing invoices

### Review Procedures

**Reasonableness Checks**:
- Compare to prior month and prior year
- Review as percentage of sales
- Investigate significant changes

**Analytical Review**:
- Do numbers make sense?
- Any unexpected relationships?
- Questions to research

**Account Review**:
- Detail review of balance sheet accounts
- Clear old items
- Verify accuracy of balances

### Common Errors to Avoid

**Timing Errors**:
- Recording in wrong period
- Missing accruals
- Not matching revenue and expense

**Classification Errors**:
- Wrong expense category
- Asset vs. expense
- Current vs. long-term

**Mathematical Errors**:
- Double entries
- Omitted transactions
- Calculation mistakes

## Working with Accountants

### Division of Responsibilities

**Typically You Handle**:
- Daily/weekly transaction entry
- Bank reconciliations
- Basic monthly close
- Preliminary statements

**Accountant May Handle**:
- Complex adjustments
- Depreciation calculations
- Tax provisions
- Final statement preparation
- Year-end close

### What to Provide

**Monthly Package**:
- Bank statements and reconciliations
- Credit card statements
- Vendor invoices
- Payroll reports
- POS sales reports
- Inventory counts

### Questions to Ask

**Understanding Your Statements**:
- Why did this account change?
- What adjustments were made?
- Are there any concerns?

**Improving Processes**:
- How can we be more efficient?
- What documentation is needed?
- Are there missing controls?

## Key Takeaways

Accurate financial statement preparation enables effective management:

1. **Follow a consistent close process**: Same steps, same timeline each month.

2. **Use a close checklist**: Ensure nothing is missed.

3. **Reconcile key accounts**: Bank, receivables, payables.

4. **Accrue expenses properly**: Match expenses to the correct period.

5. **Count inventory accurately**: Critical for cost of goods sold.

6. **Review for reasonableness**: Do the numbers make sense?

7. **Understand the connections**: P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow relate.

8. **Work effectively with accountants**: Clear communication and documentation.

---

*Develop a monthly close checklist customized for your restaurant. Establish a timeline for completing each step and producing final statements by the 15th of the following month.*

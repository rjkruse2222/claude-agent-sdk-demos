# Wine Program Profitability: Building a Successful Restaurant Wine List

A well-designed wine program can significantly boost restaurant profitability. Wine typically carries higher margins than food, and by-the-glass programs can be particularly lucrative. However, wine also ties up capital in inventory and requires knowledge to manage effectively. Strategic wine program development balances selection, pricing, and operational efficiency.

This guide covers financial aspects of building a profitable restaurant wine program.

## Wine Program Economics

### Why Wine Matters Financially

**Higher Margins**: Wine typically outperforms food margins.
- Food cost: 28-35%
- Wine by bottle: 25-35%
- Wine by glass: 15-25%

**Incremental Revenue**: Adds to check without kitchen capacity.
- No cooking required
- Storage is the only constraint
- Service is relatively quick

**Customer Experience**: Enhances dining occasion.
- Perceived value
- Celebration purchases
- Pairing opportunities

### Understanding Wine Costs

**Acquisition Cost**:
- Wholesale price from distributor
- Volume discounts may apply
- Vintage and availability affect pricing

**Carrying Cost**:
- Capital tied up in inventory
- Storage requirements
- Insurance consideration

**Service Cost**:
- Staff time for service
- Glassware costs
- Training investment

### Margin Expectations

**By Bottle**:
- Target pour cost: 30-35%
- Lower for premium wines
- Higher for value wines

**By Glass**:
- Target pour cost: 20-25%
- Varies with glass size
- Waste factor included

## Building Your Wine List

### Size Considerations

**List Size vs. Concept**:
- Quick service: 4-8 wines
- Casual dining: 15-30 wines
- Upscale casual: 30-60 wines
- Fine dining: 60+ wines

**Considerations**:
- Customer base sophistication
- Storage capacity
- Capital requirements
- Staff knowledge

### Selection Framework

**By Type** (sample casual dining):
```
Sparkling: 2-3 options
White Wine: 6-8 options
  Crisp/Light: 2-3
  Medium-bodied: 2-3
  Full/Rich: 2
Red Wine: 6-8 options
  Light-bodied: 2
  Medium-bodied: 2-3
  Full-bodied: 2-3
Rosé: 1-2 options
Dessert: 1-2 options
```

**Price Point Distribution**:
- Value tier: 25-30%
- Mid-range: 40-50%
- Premium: 20-30%

### Wine List Strategy

**Match to Menu**:
- Pair styles with cuisine
- Consider cooking methods
- Regional alignment

**Balance Familiar and Interesting**:
- Recognizable varietals for safety
- Interesting options for adventurous
- Staff can guide customers

### Supplier Relationships

**Working with Distributors**:
- Negotiate pricing
- Request samples
- Attend tastings
- Build relationships

**Multiple Suppliers**: Don't rely on one distributor.
- Better selection
- Competitive pricing
- Backup for allocation wines

## Pricing Strategies

### Standard Markup Methods

**Multiplier Method**:
```
Bottle cost × Markup = Menu price

$12 bottle × 3 = $36 menu price
```

**Common Multipliers**:
- Value wines: 3-4x
- Mid-range wines: 2.5-3x
- Premium wines: 2-2.5x

### Sliding Scale Pricing

**Rationale**: Higher-cost wines at lower markup.

**Example Scale**:
| Bottle Cost | Markup | Menu Price |
|-------------|--------|------------|
| $8-12 | 4x | $32-48 |
| $15-25 | 3x | $45-75 |
| $30-50 | 2.5x | $75-125 |
| $60+ | 2x | $120+ |

**Benefits**:
- Encourages premium purchases
- Perceived value at higher tiers
- Total dollars increase

### Competitive Pricing

**Market Research**:
- Check competitor wine lists
- Note pricing for same wines
- Position appropriately

**Avoid Embarrassment**: Same wine shouldn't be wildly different.

### Psychological Pricing

**Price Points**:
- $7, $9, $11 by glass
- $35, $45, $55 by bottle
- Avoid round numbers

**Menu Design**:
- Don't list by price order
- Feature profitable options prominently
- Use descriptions to add value

### By-the-Glass Pricing

**Basic Formula**:
```
Bottle cost ÷ Number of glasses = Cost per glass
Cost per glass ÷ Target pour cost = Glass price

$24 bottle ÷ 5 glasses = $4.80 per glass
$4.80 ÷ 0.20 = $24 per glass (20% pour cost)
```

**Reality Check**: One glass can often pay for the bottle.

## By-the-Glass Programs

### Economics of BTG

**The Math**:
- 750ml bottle = 25 ounces
- 5-ounce pour = 5 glasses per bottle

**Profit Potential**:
```
Bottle cost: $15
5 glasses at $12 each: $60 revenue
Pour cost: 25%
Gross profit: $45 per bottle
```

### Selection Strategy

**Variety**:
- Cover major categories
- Different price points
- Rotating selections

**Typical BTG Program** (casual dining):
- 2 sparkling
- 4-6 whites
- 4-6 reds
- 1-2 rosé
- 1-2 dessert

### Managing BTG Quality

**Freshness Challenges**:
- Oxidation after opening
- Quality degrades over time
- Customer expectations

**Solutions**:
- Wine preservation systems
- Argon/nitrogen preservation
- Smaller pours/larger rotation
- Track days open

### Waste Management

**Common Waste Sources**:
- Wines not sold before spoiling
- Over-pouring
- Spillage
- Dropped glasses

**Target Waste**: Under 10% of BTG sales.

**Tracking**:
- Log bottles opened
- Track glasses sold
- Calculate variance

## Inventory Management

### Inventory Levels

**Par Levels**: How much to keep on hand.

**Considerations**:
- Delivery frequency
- Storage capacity
- Capital availability
- Popular vs. slow items

**Formula**:
```
Weekly sales × Lead time + Safety stock = Par level

If selling 3 bottles/week with weekly delivery:
3 × 1 week + 2 safety = 5 bottles par
```

### Storage Requirements

**Proper Storage**:
- Temperature control (55-65°F)
- Humidity consideration
- Light protection
- Horizontal storage for cork

**Space Planning**:
- Cases vs. bottles
- Active vs. reserve storage
- Accessibility for service

### Inventory Tracking

**Count Frequency**:
- Weekly for high-volume BTG
- Monthly for full list
- Spot counts for concerns

**What to Track**:
- Quantity on hand
- Cost per bottle
- Age/vintage
- Sales velocity

### Inventory Valuation

**Methods**:
- FIFO (First In, First Out)
- Average cost
- Specific identification

**For Restaurants**: FIFO most practical.

### Turnover Analysis

**Inventory Turnover**: How fast stock sells.

**Formula**:
```
Cost of Wine Sold ÷ Average Inventory = Turnover

$100,000 annual wine cost ÷ $12,000 avg inventory = 8.3 turns
```

**Target**: 8-12 turns per year (selling every 4-6 weeks).

**Slow Movers**: Items turning less than 4x annually—consider removal.

## Wine Program ROI

### Financial Metrics

**Wine Revenue Mix**: Wine as percent of total revenue.
- Casual dining: 5-15%
- Upscale: 15-25%
- Wine-focused: 25%+

**Wine Gross Profit**:
```
Wine Revenue - Wine Cost = Gross Profit
$150,000 - $45,000 = $105,000 (70% margin)
```

**Contribution to Total Profit**: Can be significant given margins.

### Investment Requirements

**Initial Investment**:
- Starting inventory
- Storage equipment
- Glassware
- Staff training

**Ongoing Investment**:
- Inventory maintenance
- New vintages
- List updates
- Training

### Measuring Success

**Key Metrics to Track**:
- Wine revenue per guest
- Wine as % of sales
- Pour cost percentage
- Inventory turnover
- BTG waste percentage

### Staff Training ROI

**Investment**:
- Training time
- Materials
- Certifications

**Return**:
- Higher wine sales
- Better upselling
- Reduced waste
- Improved service

## Optimization Strategies

### Menu Engineering for Wine

**Analyze Each Wine**:
- Popularity (glasses/bottles sold)
- Profitability (margin)
- Classify as Star, Plowhorse, Puzzle, Dog

**Actions**:
- Stars: Feature prominently
- Plowhorses: Increase margin
- Puzzles: Promote or reposition
- Dogs: Consider removing

### Upselling Techniques

**Server Training**:
- Recommend specific wines
- Offer upgrades
- Suggest pairings
- Second glass/bottle

**Menu Design**:
- Featured wines
- Pairing suggestions
- Flight options

### Promotional Programs

**Wine Dinners**: Special events featuring wines.
- Higher-margin pricing
- Builds reputation
- Distributor support

**Happy Hour**: BTG specials.
- Increase off-peak volume
- Introduce wines
- Different margin expectations

## Key Takeaways

A profitable wine program requires strategic planning:

1. **Wine carries strong margins**: 65-80% gross margin possible.

2. **Size list appropriately**: Match to concept and capabilities.

3. **Use sliding scale pricing**: Lower markup on premium wines.

4. **BTG is highly profitable**: One glass can cover bottle cost.

5. **Manage freshness**: Preservation systems protect quality.

6. **Turn inventory efficiently**: Target 8-12 turns per year.

7. **Train staff well**: Knowledge sells wine.

8. **Track metrics**: Wine revenue per guest, pour cost, turnover.

# Restaurant Exit Strategy and Valuation: Planning for Your End Goal

Every restaurant owner should have an exit strategy—whether planning to sell in 5 years, pass to family, or build an empire. Understanding how restaurants are valued, what drives that value, and how to position for exit helps you make better decisions today. Exit planning isn't just for when you're ready to leave; it's about building a business that has value beyond you.

This guide covers restaurant exit strategies and valuation methods.

## Understanding Exit Options

### Common Exit Paths

**Sale to Third Party**:
- Individual buyer
- Restaurant group
- Private equity
- Strategic buyer (competitor, supplier)

**Management Buyout**:
- Key employees purchase business
- Often financed over time
- Maintains culture and continuity

**Family Succession**:
- Transfer to next generation
- May be gift, sale, or combination
- Estate planning considerations

**Employee Stock Ownership (ESOP)**:
- Employees own through trust
- Tax advantages possible
- Complex to establish

**Closure/Liquidation**:
- Sell assets, close business
- Last resort usually
- Sometimes best option

### Factors Affecting Exit Choice

**Consider**:
- Your financial goals
- Timeline flexibility
- Tax implications
- Legacy preferences
- Market conditions
- Business readiness

## Restaurant Valuation Methods

### Multiple of Earnings (EBITDA)

**Most Common Method**:
```
Valuation = EBITDA × Multiple

Example:
  EBITDA: $200,000
  Multiple: 3.0×
  Valuation: $600,000
```

**Typical Restaurant Multiples**:
| Business Type | Multiple Range |
|--------------|----------------|
| Single location, owner-operated | 2.0-3.0× |
| Strong single location | 3.0-4.0× |
| Multi-unit (3-10 locations) | 3.5-5.0× |
| Larger groups | 4.0-6.0× |
| High-growth concepts | 5.0-8.0× |

**Factors Affecting Multiple**:
- Growth rate
- Concept scalability
- Management depth
- Brand strength
- Location quality
- Lease terms

### Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE)

**For Smaller Restaurants**:
```
SDE = Net Income + Owner's Salary + Owner Benefits +
      Depreciation + Interest + One-Time Expenses

Example:
  Net income: $50,000
  Owner salary: $80,000
  Owner benefits: $20,000
  Depreciation: $25,000
  Interest: $15,000
  One-time expenses: $10,000
  SDE: $200,000

  Multiple: 2.5×
  Valuation: $500,000
```

### Asset-Based Valuation

**Floor Value**:
```
Asset value calculation:

Equipment (fair market): $100,000
Inventory: $15,000
Leasehold improvements: $75,000
Goodwill: Variable
Total: $190,000 + goodwill
```

**When Used**:
- Underperforming businesses
- Asset-heavy operations
- Liquidation scenarios

### Revenue Multiple

**Quick Estimate**:
```
Valuation = Annual Revenue × Multiple

Typical range: 0.3× to 0.5× revenue
(Much lower than EBITDA multiple)

Example:
  Revenue: $1,500,000
  Multiple: 0.4×
  Rough value: $600,000
```

**Limitation**: Doesn't account for profitability.

### Comparable Sales

**Market Approach**:
- What have similar restaurants sold for?
- Adjust for differences
- Consider market conditions

**Finding Comparables**:
- Business broker databases
- Industry publications
- Professional valuations

## Value Drivers

### Financial Performance

**Higher Value Indicators**:
- Strong EBITDA margins (>10%)
- Consistent revenue growth
- Stable costs
- Positive trends
- Clean financials

**Lower Value Indicators**:
- Declining sales
- Compressed margins
- High owner dependency
- Messy financials
- Recent losses

### Operational Strength

**Systems and Processes**:
```
Operational value drivers:

Strong systems add value:
  - Documented procedures
  - Trained management
  - Inventory systems
  - POS reporting
  - Quality controls

Weak systems reduce value:
  - Owner does everything
  - Tribal knowledge
  - Manual processes
  - No documentation
```

### Management and Team

**Key Question**: Can business run without owner?

**Value Premium For**:
- Strong GM in place
- Deep management bench
- Low turnover
- Training programs

### Lease Terms

**Critical Factor**:
```
Lease impact on value:

Strong lease:
  Below-market rent
  10+ years remaining
  Renewal options
  Transferable

Weak lease:
  Above-market rent
  Short term remaining
  No options
  Landlord approval required
```

### Growth Potential

**Buyers Pay More For**:
- Expansion opportunity
- Underutilized capacity
- New revenue streams
- Market growth
- Scalable concept

### Brand and Reputation

**Value Components**:
- Online reviews and ratings
- Social media presence
- Customer loyalty
- Brand recognition
- Community standing

## Exit Preparation

### Timeline

**Ideal: 3-5 Years Before Exit**:
```
Year 1-2: Foundation
  - Clean up financials
  - Document processes
  - Build management team
  - Address deferred maintenance

Year 2-3: Optimization
  - Maximize profitability
  - Reduce owner dependency
  - Strengthen brand
  - Improve operations

Year 3-5: Positioning
  - Demonstrate consistent performance
  - Show growth trajectory
  - Prepare sale materials
  - Identify potential buyers
```

### Financial Preparation

**Get Books in Order**:
- Accurate financial statements
- Separate personal expenses
- Document add-backs
- Clear categorization
- Tax returns consistent with financials

**Add-Back Documentation**:
```
Common add-backs for SDE:
  Owner salary above market: $25,000
  Personal vehicle: $8,000
  Owner health insurance: $12,000
  Family member salary (not working): $30,000
  One-time legal fees: $5,000
  Total add-backs: $80,000
```

### Operational Preparation

**Reduce Owner Dependency**:
- Hire/develop strong GM
- Document all processes
- Transfer key relationships
- Establish operating routines

**Clean Up Issues**:
- Resolve legal matters
- Address compliance issues
- Fix equipment problems
- Update décor if needed

### Documentation Package

**Due Diligence Materials**:
```
Sale documentation checklist:

Financial:
□ 3-5 years tax returns
□ 3-5 years P&L statements
□ Balance sheets
□ Cash flow statements
□ Sales by category
□ Payroll records

Operational:
□ Lease agreement
□ Equipment list
□ Vendor contracts
□ Employee information
□ Licenses and permits
□ Insurance policies

Marketing:
□ Menu and pricing
□ Brand materials
□ Customer data
□ Social media accounts
□ Reviews and ratings

Legal:
□ Corporate documents
□ Any litigation history
□ Franchise agreements (if applicable)
□ Intellectual property
```

## Transaction Process

### Valuation Process

**Getting a Valuation**:
1. Gather financial information
2. Calculate adjusted earnings
3. Research comparables
4. Apply appropriate multiples
5. Consider premiums/discounts
6. Arrive at range

**Professional Valuation**: $2,000-10,000 for formal valuation.

### Finding Buyers

**Buyer Sources**:
- Business brokers
- Industry contacts
- Employees
- Competitors
- Private equity
- Direct marketing

**Confidentiality**: Use NDA before sharing details.

### Deal Structure

**Common Structures**:
```
Asset sale (most common):
  Buyer purchases assets
  Seller keeps liabilities
  New entity formed

Stock sale:
  Buyer purchases entity
  All assets and liabilities transfer
  Less common for restaurants

Hybrid:
  Combination of cash and terms
  Seller financing common
  Earnouts possible
```

**Typical Terms**:
```
Example deal structure:

Purchase price: $600,000

Cash at closing: $400,000 (67%)
Seller note: $150,000 (25%)
  - 5-year term
  - 6% interest
  - Monthly payments
Earnout: $50,000 (8%)
  - Based on Year 1 performance
  - Paid if EBITDA exceeds $200,000
```

### Transition Planning

**Post-Sale Involvement**:
- Training period (30-90 days typical)
- Consulting arrangement
- Non-compete agreement
- Introduction to vendors/customers

### Tax Considerations

**Consult Tax Professional For**:
- Asset allocation (affects tax treatment)
- Capital gains vs. ordinary income
- Installment sale treatment
- Entity structure implications

## Key Takeaways

Exit planning should begin early and inform business decisions:

1. **Know your goal**: Define desired exit path.

2. **Understand valuation**: EBITDA multiple is primary method.

3. **Drive value**: Strong financials, systems, and management increase multiples.

4. **Reduce owner dependency**: Business must run without you.

5. **Prepare documentation**: Clean financials and organized records.

6. **Allow adequate time**: 3-5 years of preparation is ideal.

7. **Get professional help**: Brokers, accountants, attorneys add value.

8. **Structure carefully**: Deal terms affect net proceeds significantly.

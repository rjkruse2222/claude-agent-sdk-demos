# Happy Hour Pricing: Balancing Promotions and Profitability

Happy hour promotions can drive traffic during slow periods, but poorly designed discounts can erode profits. The goal isn't just to fill seats—it's to generate profitable incremental business. Strategic happy hour pricing balances attractive offers with sustainable margins, turning off-peak hours into meaningful revenue contributors.

This guide covers happy hour strategy and financial optimization.

## Happy Hour Economics

### The Purpose of Happy Hour

**Fill Empty Seats**: Generate revenue during slow periods.
- Staff is already there
- Fixed costs are running
- Incremental revenue is valuable

**Acquire Customers**: Introduce people to your restaurant.
- Trial at lower price point
- Build regular customers
- Expand awareness

**Increase Frequency**: Give regulars another reason to visit.
- Different occasion than dinner
- More affordable option
- Social gathering opportunity

### Financial Framework

**Incremental Profit Concept**:
```
Without happy hour:
  3-5 PM revenue: $200
  Costs: $150
  Profit: $50

With happy hour:
  3-5 PM revenue: $600
  Costs: $350
  Profit: $250
```

**Key Question**: Would this revenue exist without the promotion?

### Margin Trade-Offs

**Regular vs. Happy Hour**:
| Item | Regular Price | Regular Cost | HH Price | HH Cost |
|------|--------------|--------------|----------|---------|
| Draft Beer | $7 | $1.40 (20%) | $4 | $1.40 (35%) |
| Well Drink | $9 | $1.35 (15%) | $5 | $1.35 (27%) |
| Appetizer | $12 | $3.60 (30%) | $6 | $3.60 (60%) |

**Still Profitable**: Even at higher cost percentage, margin exists.

## Designing Profitable Promotions

### Types of Promotions

**Price Reductions**: Straight discount.
- $4 well drinks
- Half-price appetizers
- $3 off craft cocktails

**Special Pricing**: Different menu.
- $6 happy hour menu
- Prix fixe options
- Combo deals

**Added Value**: Same price, more value.
- Larger pours
- Free appetizer with drink purchase
- Double pour for single price

### Setting Discount Levels

**Maintain Minimum Margin**: Never sell below cost.

**Target Happy Hour Pour Cost**:
- Drinks: 25-35% (vs. 18-22% regular)
- Food: 40-50% (vs. 28-35% regular)

**Calculate Before Pricing**:
```
Regular cocktail: $11, cost $1.65 (15%)
Target HH pour cost: 30%
HH price: $1.65 ÷ 0.30 = $5.50 → $5 or $6
```

### Time and Duration

**Optimal Timing**: Gap between lunch and dinner.
- Typical: 3-6 PM or 4-7 PM
- Before dinner rush
- Enough time for turnover

**Duration Considerations**:
- Too short: Customers can't participate
- Too long: Cannibalizes dinner
- Sweet spot: 2-3 hours

### Day of Week Strategy

**When to Offer**:
- Monday-Thursday: Most common
- Slow days specific to your business
- Where you have excess capacity

**When to Avoid**:
- Friday/Saturday (already busy)
- Unless specifically slow daypart

## Pricing Strategies

### Drink Pricing

**Well/House Drinks**: Deepest discounts.
- Lowest cost products
- Can afford steeper discount
- Volume drivers

**Call/Premium**: Moderate discounts.
- Higher cost products
- Smaller discount maintains margin
- Upsell opportunity from well

**Draft Beer**: Often featured.
- High margin even at discount
- Efficient service
- Popular category

**Wine by Glass**: Attractive option.
- Strong margins
- Appeals to different crowd
- Introduce wine program

### Food Pricing

**Appetizers/Small Plates**: Common approach.
- Half-price appetizers
- $5-8 small plates menu
- Sharable items

**Special HH Menu**: Dedicated options.
- Items designed for margins
- Portions sized appropriately
- Simple for kitchen

**Upsell to Full Menu**: Transition strategy.
- HH ends but guest stays
- Full-price dinner follows
- Best of both worlds

### Combination Offers

**Drink + Food Combos**:
```
$12 combo: Any HH drink + any HH appetizer
Regular value: $7 drink + $12 app = $19
Discount: 37%
Still profitable at combined margin
```

**Benefits**:
- Increases check average
- Encourages food purchase
- Simplifies decision

## Menu Selection

### Choosing HH Drink Options

**Include**:
- High-margin items
- Fast to make
- Popular choices
- Inventory you need to move

**Avoid**:
- Low-margin specialty drinks
- Labor-intensive preparations
- Premium spirits at deep discount

### Choosing HH Food Items

**Ideal Characteristics**:
- Low food cost
- Quick preparation
- Can be prepped ahead
- Sharable format

**Good Options**:
- Fried appetizers (batch prep)
- Flatbreads/pizzas
- Wings
- Dips and spreads
- Simple tacos

**Avoid**:
- High-cost proteins
- Complex preparations
- Items that don't travel to bar

### Menu Size

**Keep It Focused**:
- 4-8 drink options
- 4-6 food options
- Easy to execute
- Quick decisions

## Measuring Success

### Key Metrics

**Traffic**:
- Guest count during HH
- Compare to pre-HH period
- Trend over time

**Revenue**:
- Total HH revenue
- Per-guest average
- % of daily revenue

**Profitability**:
- HH gross margin
- Contribution to fixed costs
- Comparison to alternatives

**Conversion**:
- HH guests staying for dinner
- HH guests returning later
- New customer acquisition

### Calculating HH Profitability

**Incremental Analysis**:
```
HH Period Performance:
  Revenue: $800
  COGS (35%): $280
  Labor (already staffed): $0 incremental
  Gross Profit: $520

Without HH (estimated):
  Revenue: $200
  COGS (30%): $60
  Gross Profit: $140

Incremental Profit from HH: $380
```

### Warning Signs

**Problems to Watch**:
- Dinner revenue declining (cannibalization)
- Same customers, lower spend
- No conversion to regular visits
- Margin below acceptable level

**Adjustments**:
- Modify timing
- Adjust pricing
- Change menu items
- Improve conversion efforts

## Operational Considerations

### Staffing

**Match to Expected Volume**:
- Adequate bar coverage
- Server/barback support
- Kitchen prep

**Shift Timing**: Consider HH when scheduling.
- Mid-shift coverage
- Avoid overtime from HH

### Service Speed

**HH Expectations**: Guests want quick service.
- Pre-batched cocktails
- Par stock set up
- Expedited food items

### Kitchen Impact

**Prep Planning**: HH food needs ready.
- Advance preparation
- Quick-fire items
- Separate station if needed

### Transition to Dinner

**Smooth Handoff**:
- Clear HH end time
- Communication to guests
- Menu transition
- Staff shift change

## Legal Considerations

### State Regulations

**Happy Hour Laws Vary By State**:
- Some states prohibit
- Some restrict what can be offered
- Advertising restrictions may apply

**Examples of Restrictions**:
- No two-for-one drinks
- No unlimited drinks
- No increasing portion for same price
- No advertising HH

**Always Check Local Laws**: Compliance is essential.

### Responsible Service

**Alcohol Service Considerations**:
- Don't encourage over-consumption
- Train staff on responsible service
- Monitor customer consumption
- Know when to cut off

## Key Takeaways

Profitable happy hour promotions drive incremental business:

1. **Focus on incremental revenue**: Fill seats that would be empty.

2. **Maintain minimum margins**: Never sell below cost.

3. **Select items strategically**: High-margin, quick-prep items.

4. **Time appropriately**: Fill slow periods, don't cannibalize dinner.

5. **Measure results**: Track traffic, revenue, and conversion.

6. **Know local laws**: Regulations vary significantly.

7. **Train staff**: Service speed and responsible alcohol service.

8. **Promote consistently**: Build awareness and regular attendance.

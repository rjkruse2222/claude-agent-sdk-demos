# Restaurant Equipment Financing: Options for Purchasing or Leasing Equipment

Restaurant equipment represents a significant capital investment—often $100,000 to $500,000 or more for a full kitchen buildout. Equipment financing helps spread this cost over time, preserving working capital for operations. Understanding your financing options enables better decisions that balance equipment needs with cash flow management.

This guide explores equipment financing options for restaurants.

## Understanding Equipment Financing

Equipment financing refers to funding specifically for purchasing or leasing business equipment, with the equipment typically serving as collateral.

### Why Finance Equipment?

**Preserve Working Capital**: Keep cash available for operations.
- Inventory purchases require cash
- Payroll must be met weekly
- Unexpected expenses need reserves

**Acquire Better Equipment**: Finance enables higher-quality purchases.
- Reliable commercial-grade equipment
- Latest technology and efficiency
- Equipment that supports growth

**Tax Benefits**: Potential deductions and credits.
- Section 179 deduction for purchased equipment
- Interest expense deductibility
- Lease payment deductions

**Match Cost to Benefit**: Pay for equipment as it generates revenue.
- Spread cost over useful life
- Align payments with cash flow
- Manage budget more predictably

### Equipment Financing vs. General Business Loans

**Equipment Financing**:
- Equipment serves as collateral
- Typically easier to qualify
- Fixed terms matching equipment life
- Rate reflects equipment's value

**General Business Loans**:
- May require additional collateral
- Harder to qualify without history
- Flexible use of funds
- Potentially higher rates for unsecured

## Equipment Loan Options

### Traditional Bank Equipment Loans

**How They Work**: Borrow funds to purchase equipment outright.

**Typical Terms**:
- Loan amounts: $25,000-$500,000+
- Terms: 3-7 years (matching equipment life)
- Interest rates: 6-12% (varies with credit)
- Down payment: 10-20% typical

**Pros**:
- Own equipment outright
- Build equity in assets
- Fixed payments for budgeting
- No restrictions on equipment use

**Cons**:
- Down payment required
- Longer approval process
- Strong credit needed
- Equipment may depreciate faster than loan

**Best For**: Established restaurants with good credit seeking long-term equipment ownership.

### SBA 7(a) Loans for Equipment

**How They Work**: Government-backed loans through approved lenders.

**Typical Terms**:
- Loan amounts: Up to $5 million
- Equipment terms: Up to 10 years (or useful life)
- Interest rates: Prime + 2.25-2.75% (variable) or fixed
- Down payment: 10-20%

**Pros**:
- Lower down payments possible
- Longer terms available
- Lower rates for qualified borrowers
- Builds banking relationship

**Cons**:
- Extensive documentation required
- Longer approval timeline (2-3 months)
- Personal guarantee required
- SBA guarantee fee

**Best For**: Restaurants purchasing significant equipment packages, especially with real estate.

### SBA 504 Loans

**How They Work**: Partnership between SBA, CDC (Certified Development Company), and lender for major purchases.

**Typical Structure**:
- Lender provides 50%
- CDC provides 40%
- Borrower provides 10%
- Minimum project: $125,000

**Pros**:
- Lower down payment (10%)
- Fixed-rate CDC portion
- Longer terms (10-20 years)
- Can include installation costs

**Cons**:
- Complex structure
- Longer timeline
- Minimum loan amounts
- Must create jobs

**Best For**: Large equipment purchases, especially combined with real estate.

### Online Lenders

**How They Work**: Technology-enabled lenders with faster processes.

**Typical Terms**:
- Loan amounts: $5,000-$500,000
- Terms: 1-5 years
- Interest rates: 8-30%+ (varies widely)
- Down payment: 0-20%

**Pros**:
- Fast approval (days, not weeks)
- Less stringent credit requirements
- Simple application process
- Flexible equipment types

**Cons**:
- Higher interest rates
- Shorter terms
- More frequent payments (often weekly)
- Less personal service

**Best For**: Quick equipment needs, newer restaurants, credit challenges.

### Vendor/Manufacturer Financing

**How They Work**: Equipment seller provides or arranges financing.

**Typical Terms**:
- Terms vary widely by vendor
- Often promotional rates (0% for period)
- May include maintenance packages
- Bundled with equipment purchase

**Pros**:
- Convenient one-stop shopping
- Promotional rates possible
- May include service agreements
- Easier approval process

**Cons**:
- May not be best rate available
- Limited to that vendor's equipment
- Promotional rates may expire
- Hidden costs in pricing

**Best For**: Single-vendor equipment purchases, promotional offers.

## Equipment Leasing Options

### Operating Lease (True Lease)

**How It Works**: Rent equipment for specific term; return at end.

**Typical Terms**:
- Terms: 2-5 years
- Payments: Lower than purchase financing
- End of lease: Return, renew, or buy at fair market value

**Pros**:
- Lower monthly payments
- Off balance sheet (depending on accounting treatment)
- Always have current equipment
- No obsolescence risk

**Cons**:
- No ownership equity
- Higher total cost if kept long-term
- Early termination penalties
- Fair market value buyout can be expensive

**Best For**: Equipment with rapid obsolescence (POS systems), short-term needs.

### Capital Lease (Finance Lease)

**How It Works**: Lease structured like a purchase; ownership transfers at end.

**Typical Terms**:
- Terms: Match useful life
- Payments: Similar to loan payments
- End of lease: $1 buyout or automatic ownership transfer

**Pros**:
- Ownership at end for nominal cost
- May require no down payment
- Fixed payments
- Tax treatment similar to ownership

**Cons**:
- On balance sheet as liability
- Responsible for maintenance
- Full commitment for term
- Less flexibility than operating lease

**Best For**: Equipment you intend to keep long-term; alternative to loan.

### Sale-Leaseback

**How It Works**: Sell equipment you own to lessor; lease it back.

**Typical Use**: Access capital from existing equipment.

**Pros**:
- Converts equity to working capital
- Keep using equipment
- Potential tax benefits
- No new equipment required

**Cons**:
- Higher total cost than keeping
- Lose ownership equity
- Ongoing payment obligation
- Equipment condition affects value

**Best For**: Cash-strapped restaurants with substantial equipment equity.

## Lease vs. Buy Analysis

### Factors to Consider

**Equipment Lifespan**: How long will you use it?
- Short lifespan: Lease may be better
- Long lifespan: Buying builds equity

**Technology Obsolescence**: Will it become outdated?
- Rapid change: Lease provides flexibility
- Stable technology: Buying makes sense

**Cash Flow**: What can you afford monthly?
- Tight cash flow: Lower lease payments help
- Strong cash flow: Buying builds assets

**Tax Situation**: What provides best tax benefit?
- Need deductions now: Section 179 on purchase
- Spread deductions: Lease payments over time

**Balance Sheet Preference**: Does debt ratio matter?
- Financing needs: Operating lease keeps debt lower
- Already healthy: Loan doesn't hurt ratios

### Sample Comparison

**Equipment Cost: $50,000**

**Loan Option**:
- 5-year term, 8% interest
- Monthly payment: $1,014
- Total paid: $60,840
- Own equipment at end

**Capital Lease**:
- 5-year term, 10% effective rate
- Monthly payment: $1,062
- Total paid: $63,720
- $1 buyout at end

**Operating Lease**:
- 5-year term
- Monthly payment: $900
- Total paid: $54,000
- Fair market value buyout: ~$8,000-$12,000
- Total if purchased: $62,000-$66,000
- Or return equipment

**Analysis**: In this example, the loan is cheapest for long-term ownership. Operating lease is cheapest if you return the equipment.

### Break-Even Analysis

Calculate how long you'd need to keep equipment for purchase to make sense:

**Purchase Total Cost** ÷ **Lease Annual Cost** = **Break-Even Years**

If equipment lasts longer than break-even, purchasing wins.

## The Application Process

### Documentation Needed

**Business Documents**:
- Business tax returns (2-3 years)
- Year-to-date profit and loss statement
- Balance sheet
- Business bank statements (3-6 months)
- Business licenses and permits

**Personal Documents**:
- Personal tax returns (2-3 years)
- Personal financial statement
- Government-issued ID
- Resume (for newer businesses)

**Equipment Information**:
- Equipment quotes or invoices
- Equipment specifications
- Vendor information
- Installation estimates

### The Approval Process

**Application Submission**: Complete lender application with documentation.

**Credit Review**: Lender evaluates business and personal credit.

**Financial Analysis**: Review of financial statements and cash flow.

**Equipment Evaluation**: Confirm equipment type and value.

**Approval Decision**: Lender provides terms or requests more information.

**Documentation**: Sign loan or lease documents.

**Funding**: Funds disbursed to vendor or borrower.

### Timeline Expectations

| Source | Typical Timeline |
|--------|-----------------|
| Online Lenders | 1-7 days |
| Bank Equipment Loans | 2-4 weeks |
| SBA Loans | 6-12 weeks |
| Vendor Financing | 1-2 weeks |
| Leasing Companies | 1-2 weeks |

### Tips for Approval

**Prepare Thoroughly**: Gather all documents before applying.

**Know Your Numbers**: Be ready to explain financials.

**Have a Plan**: Explain how equipment improves business.

**Shop Around**: Get multiple quotes to compare.

**Consider Timing**: Apply when business is performing well.

## Equipment-Specific Considerations

### Kitchen Equipment

**Major Equipment**: Ovens, ranges, fryers, refrigeration.
- High cost ($50,000-$200,000+ for full kitchen)
- Long useful life (10-15 years for quality)
- Consider financing or capital lease
- Installation costs can be included

**Ventilation/Hood Systems**: Often building improvement.
- May qualify for longer-term financing
- Could be part of leasehold improvement loan
- Consult with accountant on classification

### POS Systems

**Rapid Technology Change**: Systems evolve quickly.
- Consider operating lease for flexibility
- Subscription models increasingly common
- Factor in software costs

**Integration Importance**: System connects to many functions.
- May be worth higher cost for better integration
- Consider total cost including software and support

### Furniture and Fixtures

**Front-of-House**: Tables, chairs, booths, bar.
- Moderate lifespan (5-10 years)
- Style changes may drive replacement
- Consider whether to finance or pay cash

**Back-of-House**: Prep tables, storage, smallwares.
- Often purchased outright
- Lower individual costs
- Grouped for financing if needed

### Specialty Equipment

**Bar Equipment**: Draft systems, ice machines, refrigeration.
- Consider separately from kitchen
- Draft systems may be provided by distributors

**Coffee/Espresso**: Often high-end, brand-specific.
- Vendor financing common
- May include service agreements

**Technology**: Tablets, kiosks, display systems.
- Short lifespan, rapid obsolescence
- Lease or subscription often best

## Managing Equipment Financing

### Payment Management

**Budget Accurately**: Include all equipment payments in operating budget.

**Automate Payments**: Set up automatic payments to avoid late fees.

**Track Separately**: Know which payments go to which equipment.

**Monitor Terms**: Understand when leases expire or loans mature.

### Maintenance Responsibilities

**Loan/Capital Lease**: You're responsible for all maintenance.
- Budget for repairs
- Consider service contracts
- Maintain for resale value

**Operating Lease**: Check lease terms carefully.
- Some include maintenance
- Damage beyond normal wear charged at end
- Document equipment condition

### Insurance Requirements

**Lender Requirements**: Most require insurance on financed equipment.
- Property insurance covering equipment
- Named as loss payee
- Adequate coverage amounts

**Lease Requirements**: Similar insurance needs.
- Lessor may require specific coverage
- Compare to your existing policy

### End-of-Term Planning

**Loan Maturity**: Equipment is yours; no action needed.

**Capital Lease**: Exercise $1 buyout option.

**Operating Lease Options**:
- Return equipment
- Renew lease (often at reduced rate)
- Purchase at fair market value
- Negotiate purchase price

**Plan Ahead**: Evaluate options 6 months before term end.

## Key Takeaways

Equipment financing helps restaurants acquire necessary equipment while managing cash flow:

1. **Multiple options exist**: Loans, leases, and vendor financing each have a place.

2. **Match financing to equipment**: Short-life equipment may suit leasing; long-life suits buying.

3. **Compare total costs**: Look beyond monthly payments to total expenditure.

4. **Consider tax implications**: Section 179 for purchases; payment deductions for leases.

5. **Prepare documentation**: Financial statements, tax returns, and equipment details needed.

6. **Shop multiple sources**: Banks, online lenders, and leasing companies offer different terms.

7. **Plan for the long term**: Consider equipment replacement when original financing ends.

8. **Factor in all costs**: Installation, maintenance, and insurance add to equipment cost.

*Identify your upcoming equipment needs and estimated costs. Compare financing options from multiple sources. Calculate total cost of ownership under each scenario before deciding.*

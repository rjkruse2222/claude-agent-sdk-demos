# Restaurant Buildout Costs: Budgeting for Construction and Renovation

Restaurant buildout is typically the largest single investment in opening a new location. Whether building from shell space or renovating an existing restaurant, understanding buildout costs helps create realistic budgets and avoid the financial pitfalls that derail many restaurant projects.

This guide breaks down restaurant buildout costs and provides budgeting guidance.

## Understanding Restaurant Buildout

### What Is Restaurant Buildout?

**Buildout** encompasses all construction, renovation, and preparation required to make a space restaurant-ready.

**Types of Buildout**:
- **Shell Space**: Raw space with nothing—requires everything
- **Second-Generation**: Previous restaurant space—modify existing
- **Renovation**: Update existing owned space
- **Ground-Up**: New construction building

### Key Cost Drivers

**Space Condition**: What exists vs. what's needed.
- Shell = highest cost, most flexibility
- Second-gen = lower cost if systems exist

**Concept Type**: Service style affects requirements.
- Full-service = more front-of-house investment
- Quick-service = more kitchen efficiency focus
- Bar = beverage system investment

**Location Factors**: Geography matters.
- Urban construction more expensive
- Regional labor and material costs vary
- Code requirements differ by jurisdiction

**Quality Level**: Build quality impacts cost.
- Budget finishes vs. premium materials
- Basic vs. custom millwork
- Standard vs. specialty equipment

## Cost Categories Breakdown

### Architectural and Design

**Architecture**: Building design and construction documents.
- Typical cost: $15,000-$50,000
- Based on complexity and size
- Required for permits

**Interior Design**: Look and feel of space.
- Typical cost: $10,000-$30,000
- Furniture, fixtures, finishes specification
- May overlap with architect

**Engineering**: Structural, MEP (mechanical, electrical, plumbing).
- Often included with architectural
- Separate structural if major changes

**Design Total**: 5-10% of overall buildout cost.

### Construction - Hard Costs

**Demolition**: Removing existing elements.
- Range: $5-$15 per square foot
- Higher if hazardous materials present
- Depends on existing condition

**Framing and Walls**: Building structure.
- Range: $10-$30 per square foot
- Depends on layout complexity
- Includes drywall and finishing

**Flooring**: All floor surfaces.
- Kitchen: $8-$15/SF (quarry tile, epoxy)
- Dining: $5-$25/SF (tile, wood, concrete)
- Bar: $10-$30/SF (often specialty)

**Ceilings**: Overhead finishes.
- Drop ceiling: $3-$6/SF
- Drywall: $5-$10/SF
- Exposed/industrial: $2-$5/SF plus paint

**Plumbing**: All water and drainage.
- Range: $15,000-$75,000
- Kitchen sink requirements significant
- Restroom construction
- Grease trap installation: $5,000-$15,000

**Electrical**: Power and lighting.
- Range: $20,000-$100,000
- Kitchen equipment demands significant
- Lighting design and fixtures
- Panel upgrades if needed

**HVAC**: Heating, ventilation, air conditioning.
- Range: $25,000-$100,000+
- Kitchen make-up air significant
- Dining comfort important
- May require rooftop units

**Hood and Ventilation**: Kitchen exhaust.
- Range: $15,000-$50,000
- Size depends on cooking equipment
- Fire suppression included
- Heavily regulated

**Fire Suppression**: Sprinklers and systems.
- Range: $5,000-$30,000
- Often required by code
- May be modified from existing

### Finishes and Fixtures

**Millwork**: Custom built-ins.
- Host stand, bar, booths: $20,000-$100,000+
- Custom vs. stock dramatically different
- Major design impact

**Bar Construction**: If applicable.
- Range: $30,000-$150,000+
- Includes plumbing, refrigeration
- Major cost center for bar concepts

**Paint and Wallcoverings**: Interior finishes.
- Range: $5,000-$20,000
- Varies with quality and complexity

**Signage**: Interior and exterior.
- Range: $5,000-$30,000
- Exterior sign often regulated
- Menu boards, wayfinding

### Furniture, Fixtures, and Equipment (FF&E)

**Kitchen Equipment**: The heart of operations.
- Range: $75,000-$300,000+
- Cooking equipment: $30,000-$100,000
- Refrigeration: $20,000-$60,000
- Prep tables, racks: $10,000-$30,000
- Dishwashing: $5,000-$25,000
- Smallwares: $10,000-$30,000

**Bar Equipment**: Beverage service.
- Draft system: $10,000-$40,000
- Bar refrigeration: $10,000-$30,000
- Ice machines: $5,000-$15,000
- Glassware/bar tools: $5,000-$15,000

**Dining Furniture**: Tables and chairs.
- Range: $15,000-$75,000
- $200-$400 per seat typical
- Quality varies significantly

**Point of Sale**: Technology infrastructure.
- Hardware: $5,000-$20,000
- Includes terminals, printers, network

### Soft Costs

**Permits and Fees**: Required approvals.
- Building permit: $5,000-$20,000
- Health department: $500-$2,000
- Liquor license: $1,000-$100,000+ (varies by state)
- Other inspections and fees

**Professional Fees**: Beyond design.
- General contractor management
- Expediter (in complex markets)
- Legal review

**Pre-Opening**: Before doors open.
- Initial inventory: $10,000-$30,000
- Staff training: $10,000-$25,000
- Marketing launch: $5,000-$20,000
- Working capital reserve

## Budgeting by Restaurant Type

### Quick-Service Restaurant

**Size**: 1,500-2,500 SF typical

**Cost Range**: $250,000-$500,000

**Budget Breakdown**:
- Construction: 40-50%
- Kitchen equipment: 25-35%
- FF&E and technology: 10-15%
- Soft costs: 10-15%

**Key Considerations**:
- Efficient kitchen is priority
- Less dining room investment
- Drive-thru adds significant cost

### Fast-Casual Restaurant

**Size**: 2,000-3,500 SF typical

**Cost Range**: $400,000-$750,000

**Budget Breakdown**:
- Construction: 40-45%
- Kitchen equipment: 20-30%
- Dining FF&E: 15-20%
- Soft costs: 10-15%

**Key Considerations**:
- Balance kitchen and dining investment
- Design differentiation important
- Technology integration (ordering kiosks)

### Casual Dining Restaurant

**Size**: 3,000-5,000 SF typical

**Cost Range**: $500,000-$1,000,000

**Budget Breakdown**:
- Construction: 40-50%
- Kitchen equipment: 20-25%
- Dining FF&E and decor: 20-25%
- Soft costs: 10-15%

**Key Considerations**:
- Dining atmosphere investment
- Full bar if included adds cost
- Larger kitchen for full menu

### Fine Dining Restaurant

**Size**: 2,500-5,000 SF typical

**Cost Range**: $750,000-$2,000,000+

**Budget Breakdown**:
- Construction and finishes: 45-55%
- Kitchen equipment: 15-20%
- Dining FF&E and custom elements: 25-30%
- Soft costs: 10-15%

**Key Considerations**:
- Premium finishes throughout
- Custom millwork and design
- Wine storage and service equipment

### Bar/Nightclub

**Size**: 2,000-5,000 SF typical

**Cost Range**: $400,000-$1,500,000+

**Budget Breakdown**:
- Construction: 35-45%
- Bar build-out and equipment: 25-35%
- Sound/lighting/AV: 15-20%
- Soft costs: 10-15%

**Key Considerations**:
- Bar construction major investment
- Sound and lighting critical
- Restroom capacity for volume

## Cost Control Strategies

### Value Engineering

**Definition**: Achieving required function at lowest cost.

**Approaches**:
- Alternative materials with same look
- Standard vs. custom elements
- Phased improvements (open basic, upgrade later)

**Examples**:
- Concrete floors vs. tile (similar look, lower cost)
- Stock fixtures vs. custom (functional, less unique)
- Reclaimed materials (character at lower cost)

### Second-Generation Spaces

**Advantages**:
- Kitchen infrastructure exists
- Hood and ventilation in place
- Plumbing and electrical roughed in
- May have existing permits

**Savings Potential**: 30-50% vs. shell space

**Watch Out For**:
- Deferred maintenance issues
- Code compliance requirements
- Equipment that doesn't fit your menu

### Equipment Decisions

**New vs. Used Equipment**:
- Used: 30-50% savings
- Risk: Unknown history, shorter life
- Best for: Refrigeration, prep tables

**Lease vs. Buy**:
- Lease: Lower upfront cost
- Preserves capital for other needs
- Higher total cost over time

**Phased Equipment**:
- Start with essentials
- Add specialty equipment as volume justifies

### Contractor Selection

**Competitive Bidding**:
- Minimum three bids
- Detailed scope for comparison
- Check references thoroughly

**Restaurant Experience**:
- Experienced contractors know restaurant needs
- Avoid learning curve on your project
- May cost slightly more but worth it

**Fixed Price vs. Cost Plus**:
- Fixed price: Cost certainty
- Cost plus: Flexibility but less predictable

## Financing Buildout Costs

### SBA Loans

**504 Loan**:
- Up to 90% financing
- Long terms (10-25 years)
- Includes real estate and equipment
- Lower rates

**7(a) Loan**:
- Flexible use
- Up to $5 million
- Can finance buildout costs
- Good for smaller projects

### Landlord Contributions

**Tenant Improvement Allowance (TI)**:
- Landlord contributes to buildout
- Range: $20-$100+ per SF
- Built into lease terms
- May increase rent

**Build-to-Suit**:
- Landlord builds to your spec
- You lease finished space
- Higher rent reflects investment
- Less capital required

### Equipment Financing

**Equipment Loans**:
- Finance equipment separately
- Equipment as collateral
- Preserve capital

**Lease Options**:
- Operating lease: Return at end
- Capital lease: Own at end
- Spread cost over time

### Investor Capital

**Equity Investment**:
- Investors provide capital
- No debt repayment
- Give up ownership percentage
- May want involvement

**Convertible Notes**:
- Starts as debt
- Converts to equity
- Bridge financing option

## Common Budget Mistakes

### Underestimating Costs

**The Problem**: Initial budgets often 20-40% low.

**Common Misses**:
- Permit and fee costs
- Engineering requirements
- Change orders during construction
- Pre-opening expenses

**Solution**: Add 15-20% contingency to budget.

### Scope Creep

**The Problem**: Project grows during construction.

**Examples**:
- "While we're at it..." additions
- Design changes mid-project
- Discovering hidden problems

**Solution**: Complete plans before starting; formal change order process.

### Timing Mistakes

**The Problem**: Delays cost money.

**Impacts**:
- Rent during construction
- Staff hired before opening
- Loan interest accruing
- Lost revenue opportunity

**Solution**: Realistic timeline; buffer for delays.

### Overlooking Soft Costs

**The Problem**: Focus on construction, forget the rest.

**Forgotten Items**:
- Legal and accounting fees
- Insurance during construction
- Initial inventory
- Marketing launch
- Working capital

**Solution**: Comprehensive budget including all categories.

## Project Management Tips

### Pre-Construction Planning

**Complete Design First**:
- Finalize plans before construction
- Reduce change orders
- Enable accurate bidding

**Permit Timeline**:
- Start permit process early
- Can take weeks to months
- Don't assume quick approval

**Lead Time Items**:
- Order custom items early
- Equipment with long lead times
- Special materials or fixtures

### During Construction

**Regular Site Visits**:
- Weekly at minimum
- Catch issues early
- Maintain communication

**Document Everything**:
- Photos of progress
- Written change orders
- Meeting notes

**Payment Schedule**:
- Tie payments to milestones
- Retain percentage until completion
- Don't get ahead of work

### Budget Tracking

**Track Commitments**:
- Know what's contracted vs. spent
- Update budget weekly
- Flag variances immediately

**Contingency Management**:
- Use contingency reluctantly
- Require approval for draws
- Replenish if possible

## Key Takeaways

Restaurant buildout requires careful planning and realistic budgeting:

1. **Costs vary widely**: $200-$600+ per SF depending on concept and condition.

2. **Second-gen spaces save money**: 30-50% less than shell space if infrastructure exists.

3. **Budget all categories**: Construction, FF&E, equipment, and soft costs.

4. **Include contingency**: 15-20% for unexpected costs.

5. **Plan before building**: Complete design reduces change orders.

6. **Consider financing options**: SBA loans, landlord TI, equipment financing.

7. **Track budget closely**: Weekly updates during construction.

8. **Don't forget soft costs**: Permits, pre-opening, working capital.

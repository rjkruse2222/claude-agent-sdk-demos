# Restaurant Equipment Costs: Buy, Lease, or Finance?

Restaurant equipment represents one of the largest capital investments in opening or renovating a restaurant. The average restaurant spends $100,000-$300,000 on equipment, with high-end concepts investing significantly more. How you acquire this equipment—buying outright, leasing, or financing—significantly impacts your cash flow, balance sheet, and long-term costs.

This guide helps you evaluate the buy, lease, and finance options to make the best decisions for your restaurant's situation.

## Understanding Restaurant Equipment Costs

Before evaluating acquisition methods, understand the full scope of equipment investments.

### Major Equipment Categories

**Cooking Equipment**
- Ranges and ovens: $2,000-$20,000
- Fryers: $1,000-$8,000
- Grills and griddles: $1,000-$10,000
- Salamanders and broilers: $2,000-$8,000
- Steam equipment: $3,000-$15,000

**Refrigeration**
- Walk-in coolers: $5,000-$20,000
- Walk-in freezers: $8,000-$25,000
- Reach-in refrigerators: $2,000-$8,000
- Prep tables: $1,500-$5,000
- Display cases: $2,000-$10,000

**Food Preparation**
- Mixers: $500-$10,000
- Food processors: $500-$3,000
- Slicers: $500-$4,000
- Prep tables and sinks: $1,000-$5,000

**Warewashing**
- Dishwashers: $3,000-$25,000
- Glasswashers: $2,000-$8,000
- Three-compartment sinks: $1,000-$3,000

**Front of House**
- POS systems: $5,000-$30,000
- Furniture: $10,000-$100,000+
- Bar equipment: $10,000-$50,000
- Display and décor: Varies widely

### Beyond Purchase Price

Total equipment cost includes:

**Installation**: Can add 10-25% to equipment cost.

**Ventilation**: Hood systems often cost more than cooking equipment beneath them.

**Electrical and Plumbing**: Infrastructure modifications.

**Permits**: Equipment-related permits and inspections.

**Training**: Learning to operate new equipment.

**Maintenance**: Ongoing service and repair costs.

## Option 1: Buying Equipment Outright

Purchasing equipment with cash provides ownership and control but requires significant upfront capital.

### Advantages of Buying

**Total Ownership**: The equipment is yours with no ongoing payments.

**Lowest Total Cost**: No interest or fees; buying outright is typically cheapest long-term.

**No Restrictions**: Use, modify, or sell as you see fit.

**Tax Benefits**: Potential Section 179 deductions for immediate write-off.

**Negotiating Power**: Cash buyers often negotiate better prices.

**No Credit Impact**: Doesn't affect borrowing capacity.

### Disadvantages of Buying

**Cash Requirements**: Large upfront capital outlay.

**Opportunity Cost**: Cash used for equipment can't be used elsewhere.

**Obsolescence Risk**: You bear the risk if equipment becomes obsolete.

**Maintenance Responsibility**: All maintenance and repair costs are yours.

**Depreciation**: Asset value declines over time.

### When Buying Makes Sense

**Strong Cash Position**: Adequate capital reserves after purchase.

**Stable Operations**: Established restaurant with predictable needs.

**Long-Term Equipment**: Items with long lifespans like refrigeration.

**Used Equipment**: Significant discounts for quality used items.

**Simple Equipment**: Items unlikely to need upgrades.

## Option 2: Leasing Equipment

Leasing provides use of equipment without ownership, with monthly payments over a set term.

### Types of Equipment Leases

**Operating Lease**
- Lower monthly payments
- Equipment returned at end of term
- May include maintenance
- Off-balance-sheet treatment (changing under new accounting standards)
- Best for equipment with short useful life or rapid obsolescence

**Capital Lease (Finance Lease)**
- Higher monthly payments
- Ownership typically transfers at end
- Treated as purchase for accounting and tax purposes
- Often includes purchase option at lease end
- Best when you want to own eventually

### Advantages of Leasing

**Preserve Capital**: No large upfront payment.

**Predictable Costs**: Fixed monthly payments aid budgeting.

**Flexibility**: Upgrade equipment at lease end.

**Maintenance Inclusion**: Some leases include service.

**Easier Qualification**: May be easier to obtain than loans.

**Tax Treatment**: Operating lease payments may be fully deductible.

### Disadvantages of Leasing

**Higher Total Cost**: Leasing typically costs more than buying over time.

**No Ownership**: You don't build equity in the equipment.

**Restrictions**: May have usage or modification restrictions.

**Commitment**: Locked into payments for lease term.

**End-of-Lease Costs**: Potential fees for damage, early termination, or purchase.

### When Leasing Makes Sense

**Limited Capital**: Need equipment but cash is tight.

**Rapidly Changing Technology**: POS systems, digital signage.

**Uncertain Long-Term Needs**: Testing concepts or locations.

**Included Services**: When maintenance inclusion has value.

**Short-Term Projects**: Pop-ups, temporary locations.

## Option 3: Financing Equipment

Equipment financing provides borrowed funds to purchase equipment, with the equipment serving as collateral.

### Types of Equipment Financing

**Equipment Loans**
- Borrow to purchase equipment
- Equipment serves as collateral
- You own from day one
- Fixed or variable interest rates
- Terms typically 3-7 years

**SBA Loans**
- Government-backed loans for small businesses
- 7(a) loans: Up to $5 million for general purposes
- 504 loans: For major equipment and real estate
- Lower down payments, longer terms
- More documentation required

**Equipment Lines of Credit**
- Revolving credit for equipment purchases
- Draw as needed up to limit
- Flexibility for multiple purchases
- May have variable rates

### Advantages of Financing

**Ownership**: You own the equipment while making payments.

**Tax Benefits**: Depreciation and interest deductions.

**Build Equity**: Payments build ownership stake.

**Preserve Relationships**: Work with your bank.

**Predictable Payments**: Fixed rates provide payment certainty.

### Disadvantages of Financing

**Interest Cost**: You pay more than purchase price over time.

**Qualification Requirements**: Credit, collateral, and documentation required.

**Monthly Obligation**: Payments required regardless of business performance.

**Collateral Risk**: Equipment can be repossessed if payments missed.

**Personal Guarantees**: May be required, especially for new businesses.

### When Financing Makes Sense

**Moderate Capital**: Have some cash but not enough to buy outright.

**Growth Investment**: Equipment that will generate returns.

**Long-Term Assets**: Items you'll use for years.

**Tax Planning**: When depreciation benefits are valuable.

**Building Credit**: Establishing business credit history.

## Equipment-Specific Considerations

Different equipment types favor different acquisition methods.

### High-Value, Long-Life Equipment

Examples: Walk-in coolers, heavy-duty ranges, ventilation systems.

**Recommendation**: Buy or finance.

**Rationale**: Long useful life means you'll likely use throughout any loan or lease term. Ownership makes sense. Consider used equipment for better value.

### Technology Equipment

Examples: POS systems, digital displays, ordering kiosks.

**Recommendation**: Lease or short-term finance.

**Rationale**: Technology changes rapidly. Leasing provides upgrade flexibility. Avoid being stuck with obsolete systems.

### Commodity Equipment

Examples: Basic prep tables, shelving, smallwares.

**Recommendation**: Buy outright.

**Rationale**: Low cost makes leasing/financing overhead disproportionate. Buy quality used when possible.

### Specialized Equipment

Examples: Specialty cooking equipment, custom fabrication.

**Recommendation**: Buy or finance.

**Rationale**: Specialized items have limited resale/return value, making leasing less favorable. Ensure it meets your specific needs before committing.

### Used Equipment

**Recommendation**: Buy outright when quality is verified.

**Rationale**: Significant savings (50-70% off new prices) but limited warranty. Have inspected by technician. Not typically available for lease.

## The Decision Framework

Use this framework to evaluate acquisition options for each equipment purchase.

### Key Questions

**What's Your Cash Position?**
- Abundant cash: Buying often best
- Limited cash: Leasing or financing necessary
- Moderate cash: Consider financing to preserve reserves

**How Long Will You Need It?**
- 5+ years: Buying or financing favored
- 2-5 years: Financing or leasing
- Under 2 years: Leasing favored

**How Quickly Does It Change?**
- Stable technology: Buying or financing
- Rapidly evolving: Leasing favored

**What's the Equipment's Role?**
- Critical operations: Consider leasing with maintenance
- Secondary: Buy outright, especially used

### Total Cost Comparison

For any significant purchase, calculate total cost under each option:

**Buy Total Cost**
= Purchase Price + Installation + Maintenance over life

**Lease Total Cost**
= (Monthly Payment × Months) + End-of-lease costs + Excess use fees

**Finance Total Cost**
= Down Payment + (Monthly Payment × Months) + Interest + Maintenance

Compare net present value if sophisticated analysis is warranted.

### Decision Matrix

| Factor | Favor Buy | Favor Lease | Favor Finance |
|--------|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Cash available | Abundant | Limited | Moderate |
| Equipment life | Long | Short | Long |
| Technology change | Slow | Fast | Slow |
| Need flexibility | No | Yes | No |
| Tax situation | High income | Moderate | High income |
| Business stage | Established | New/uncertain | Growing |

## Tax Implications

Tax treatment significantly affects effective cost of each option.

### Buying (Section 179)

**Section 179 Deduction**: Deduct full purchase price in year of purchase (limits apply).

**Bonus Depreciation**: Additional depreciation for qualifying property.

**MACRS Depreciation**: If not using Section 179, depreciate over useful life.

**Best For**: Profitable businesses seeking immediate tax reduction.

### Leasing Tax Treatment

**Operating Lease**: Payments generally fully deductible as business expense.

**Capital Lease**: Treated similarly to purchase for tax purposes.

**Best For**: Businesses wanting to spread deductions over time.

### Financing Tax Treatment

**Interest Deduction**: Loan interest is deductible.

**Depreciation**: Depreciate asset as if purchased.

**Best For**: Businesses wanting both interest and depreciation deductions.

### Consult Professionals

Tax laws are complex and change frequently. Consult with a tax professional familiar with restaurant businesses before making significant equipment decisions.

## Working with Vendors and Lenders

Effective negotiation improves terms under any acquisition method.

### Negotiating Purchases

**Get Multiple Quotes**: Price shopping is expected.

**Ask for Package Deals**: Buying multiple items can yield discounts.

**Negotiate Installation**: Often has significant markup.

**Consider Timing**: End of month/quarter/year may yield better deals.

**Explore Used Options**: Ask about refurbished or demo equipment.

### Working with Lessors

**Compare Multiple Lessors**: Terms vary significantly.

**Understand All Fees**: Application, documentation, end-of-lease, etc.

**Negotiate Purchase Options**: Buyout terms at lease end.

**Review Maintenance Terms**: What's included vs. your responsibility.

**Understand Early Termination**: Penalties and procedures.

### Securing Equipment Financing

**Prepare Documentation**: Financial statements, tax returns, business plan.

**Shop Multiple Lenders**: Banks, credit unions, SBA lenders, online lenders.

**Compare True Costs**: APR, fees, prepayment penalties.

**Consider Relationship Value**: Your bank may offer better terms.

**Understand Collateral Requirements**: Equipment vs. additional collateral.

## Key Takeaways

Equipment acquisition decisions significantly impact your restaurant's finances:

1. **Understand full costs**: Consider purchase price, installation, maintenance, and total cost over useful life.

2. **Match method to equipment type**: Technology favors leasing; long-life equipment favors buying or financing.

3. **Consider your cash position**: Preserve capital for operations; don't overextend on equipment.

4. **Calculate total cost**: Compare options using total cost over the equipment's useful life.

5. **Factor in tax implications**: Different methods have different tax treatments; consult professionals.

6. **Negotiate everything**: Whether buying, leasing, or financing, better terms are available to those who ask.

7. **Consider used equipment**: Quality used items can provide significant savings when buying outright.

8. **Plan for maintenance**: Factor ongoing costs into your analysis regardless of acquisition method.

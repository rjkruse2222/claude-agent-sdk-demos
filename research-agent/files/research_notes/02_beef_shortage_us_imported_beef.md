# Beef Shortage in the United States: Causes, Concerns, and Differences from Imported Beef

**Research Date:** November 18, 2025
**Sources:** USDA data, industry reports 2024-2025

## Causes of Beef Shortage

### 1. Drought and Climate Factors
- **Primary Factor:** Severe supply shortage
- U.S. cattle herd at **smallest level since 1951**
- Multiyear droughts persisting since 2020
- Widespread water and forage shortages

**Most Affected Regions:**
- Texas
- Oklahoma
- Kansas

**Impact:** Drought increased feed costs and forced producers to send cattle for early slaughter

### 2. Herd Contraction
- U.S. calf crop down an estimated **600,000 head in 2024**
- Smaller decline expected in 2025 (possibly marking cycle low)
- Contributing factors:
  - Higher producer input costs
  - Supply chain issues
  - Ongoing drought conditions

## Key Concerns

### Production Declines
- **2025 Projection:** U.S. beef production to decline to 11.81 million metric tons (mmt)
- Represents **3.98% decrease** from 2024 estimate of 12.3 mmt
- Overall decline of nearly **600 million pounds or 2.1%** for the year

### Price Increases
- Beef and veal expected to increase by **3.2% in 2025**
- Prices expected to reach record highs
- Approaching nearly **$10/lb**
- March 2024 data showed **7.7% price increase** for meat, poultry, fish, and eggs

### Slow Recovery Timeline
- Industry experts expect meat supply to stay tight well into 2025 and possibly beyond
- **Critical Point:** Restocking cattle herds takes **years, not months**
- Long-term supply constraints expected

## Differences Between US Beef and Imported Beef

### Quality Differences

**US Beef:**
- Typically **"grain-fed"** and higher quality
- Already differentiated as high quality compared to most other countries
- Produces leaner beef with more intramuscular fat

**Imported Beef:**
- Majority is lower quality **"grass-fed"** beef
- Generally leaner with less marbling
- Quality difference between exported and imported beef is significant

### Value Gap
- Peak value difference: Nearly **70 cents per pound** in 2018
- U.S. exports higher-quality, higher-value beef
- U.S. imports lower-cost lean trim

### What the US Imports

**Primary Imports:**
- Nearly **70% are lean beef trimmings**
- Mainly from Australia and New Zealand
- Frozen 90% lean trim
- Blended with U.S. fat trim into 80/20 grinds
- Used in ground beef products

### Safety Standards

**Rigorous Requirements:**
- All imported meat must be presented for FSIS inspection
- Exporting countries must pass rigorous USDA audit process
- Must prove food safety systems are equivalent to US standards
- Maintains consumer safety across imports

### Grading System Differences

**USDA System:**
- Three quality categories: Prime, Choice, and Select
- Emphasizes intramuscular fat
- Found in greater amounts in grain-fed beef

**International Variance:**
- Different countries use different grading standards
- Systems not directly comparable
- US system favors marbling and grain-feeding practices

## Summary

The U.S. beef shortage is driven by climate-related herd reductions and will persist for years due to the slow nature of cattle herd rebuilding. While the U.S. produces high-quality grain-fed beef, it imports leaner grass-fed trimmings from countries like Australia and New Zealand to supplement domestic production, particularly for ground beef products. Despite quality differences, all imported beef must meet equivalent safety standards to domestic production.
